Obstetrics 478 questions

Antenatal care (ANC)

Must-doRepeated59 past questions

At least 4 antenatal visits (8 contacts in the WHO model). Routine: Hb, blood group and Rh, HIV, syphilis, HBsAg, urine, OGTT, TT/Td, iron-folic acid from the second trimester. Recurrent second-trimester losses point to cervical incompetence or a uterine anomaly: check the cervix and uterus by ultrasound.

Try a past question

A female comes to OPD-Gyne for pre-conceptional counselling, with history of two second trimester abortions. What is the next investigation you will advice?

  1. A. Hysteroscopy
  2. B. Endometrial biopsy
  3. C. Chromosomal abnormalities
  4. D. TVS

Answer: D. Two second-trimester losses suggest cervical incompetence or a uterine anomaly, which a transvaginal scan shows.

Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy

Must-doRepeated46 past questions

Pre-eclampsia: BP 140/90 or more after 20 weeks with proteinuria or organ involvement. Magnesium sulfate prevents and treats eclampsia (watch knee jerks, breathing, urine; antidote calcium gluconate). Labetalol and nifedipine control BP. Delivery is the only cure: deliver severe disease at 34 weeks or more once stable.

Try a past question

A 28-year-old primigravida at 34 weeks presents with blood pressure 170/110 mmHg, severe headache, visual disturbances, and epigastric pain. Urine shows 3+ protein. What is the most definitive management?

  1. A. Continue pregnancy with antihypertensives
  2. B. Magnesium sulfate only
  3. C. Bed rest and observation
  4. D. Immediate delivery after stabilization

Answer: D. Severe pre-eclampsia at 34 weeks: stabilise with magnesium and antihypertensives, then deliver.

Labor & partograph

Must-doRepeated112 past questions

Know the partograph: the alert and action lines (WHO), and that the active phase is now counted from 5 cm (WHO 2018 labour care guide). Stages: first (to full dilatation), second (to birth), third (to placenta, managed actively with oxytocin). Sudden loss of contractions, bleeding and lost fetal heart in a scarred uterus means rupture: laparotomy.

Try a past question

A 32-year-old G2P1 with a previous LSCS is in labor at 38 weeks. She develops sudden vaginal bleeding with loss of uterine contractions, and the FHR cannot be auscultated. What is the next best management option?

  1. A. Vacuum delivery
  2. B. Emergency laparotomy
  3. C. Blood transfusion
  4. D. Urgent ultrasound

Answer: B. Bleeding, loss of contractions and an absent fetal heart after a previous caesarean mean uterine rupture, which needs immediate laparotomy.

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)

Must-doRepeated49 past questions

PPH: blood loss of 500 mL or more after vaginal birth (1,000 mL after caesarean). Causes, the 4 Ts: tone (atony, the commonest), trauma, tissue, thrombin. Atony: massage, oxytocin, then ergometrine, carboprost, misoprostol; tranexamic acid within 3 hours; then balloon tamponade or surgery.

Try a past question

A 26-year-old woman develops severe vaginal bleeding immediately after delivery. Examination reveals a soft, enlarged uterus despite an empty bladder. What is the most likely cause of this postpartum hemorrhage?

  1. A. Genital tract trauma
  2. B. Uterine atony
  3. C. Retained placental tissue
  4. D. Uterine inversion

Answer: B. A soft, boggy uterus after delivery is uterine atony, the commonest cause of PPH.

Fetal monitoring: CTG, NST, biophysical profile, Doppler

Must-doRepeated32 past questions

CTG: early decelerations = head compression (benign); variable = cord compression; late = uteroplacental insufficiency (worrying). Reactive NST: 2 accelerations in 20 minutes. Growth restriction is followed with umbilical artery Doppler; absent or reversed end-diastolic flow is serious.

  3. C. Fetal hypoxia
  4. D. Uteroplacental insufficiency

Answer: B. Variable decelerations, changing in timing and shape, come from cord compression.

Obstetric emergencies: shoulder dystocia, cord prolapse, uterine rupture, amniotic fluid embolism

Must-doRepeated49 past questions

Shoulder dystocia: call for help, McRoberts with suprapubic pressure first; never fundal pressure. Cord prolapse: lift the presenting part off the cord, knee-chest position, urgent delivery. Amniotic fluid embolism: sudden collapse, hypoxia and DIC in labour.

Try a past question

A 30-year-old woman with gestational diabetes delivers the fetal head vaginally. The head retracts against the perineum, and gentle axial traction fails to deliver the shoulders. Which is the most appropriate initial maneuver?

  1. A. Apply strong downward traction on the fetal head
  2. B. Perform the McRoberts maneuver with suprapubic pressure as appropriate
  3. C. Apply fundal pressure to force the anterior shoulder beneath the pubic symphysis
  4. D. Immediately perform forceful internal rotation of the fetal head

Answer: B. McRoberts manoeuvre with suprapubic pressure is the first step for shoulder dystocia; fundal pressure and strong traction make it worse.

Antepartum hemorrhage: placenta previa, abruption

High-yieldRepeated38 past questions

Placenta previa: painless, recurrent bright bleeding, soft uterus; never do a vaginal examination before a scan. Abruption: painful bleeding, tense tender uterus, fetal distress; linked to pre-eclampsia, trauma and cocaine; can cause DIC.

Try a past question

A 26-year-old woman at 36 weeks presents with vaginal bleeding and painful uterine contractions. The uterus is tender and firm, and fetal heart rate shows abnormalities. Which diagnosis is most likely?

  1. A. Placenta previa
  2. B. Placental abruption
  3. C. Cervical polyp
  4. D. Vaginal candidiasis

Answer: B. Painful bleeding with a tense, tender uterus and fetal distress is placental abruption.

Multiple pregnancy

High-yieldRepeated24 past questions

Chorionicity is decided best on a first-trimester scan: lambda sign = dichorionic, T sign = monochorionic. Twin-to-twin transfusion happens only in monochorionic twins. After the first twin, a transverse second twin is delivered by internal podalic version and breech extraction.

Answer: C. A transverse second twin after vaginal delivery of the first is turned by internal podalic version.

Gestational diabetes mellitus

High-yieldRepeated24 past questions

India (DIPSI): one-step 75 g glucose, 2-hour value 140 mg/dL or more = GDM, done fasting or not. IADPSG cut-offs: fasting 92, 1 hour 180, 2 hours 153; any one value is enough. First treatment is diet and exercise, then insulin or metformin. Babies: macrosomia, hypoglycaemia, shoulder dystocia.

Try a past question

A 28-year-old primigravida at 28 weeks undergoes a 75g oral glucose tolerance test. Her values are: Fasting 96 mg/dL, 1 hour 184 mg/dL, 2 hour 156 mg/dl. What is the most appropriate diagnosis?

  1. A. Normal pregnancy
  2. B. Overt diabetes mellitus
  3. C. Impaired fasting glucose only
  4. D. Gestational diabetes mellitus

Answer: D. A fasting value of 96 and a 2-hour value of 156 each cross the IADPSG cut-offs (92 and 153): gestational diabetes.

Rh isoimmunization

High-yieldRepeated20 past questions

Give anti-D within 72 hours of delivery to an Rh-negative mother with an Rh-positive baby whose indirect Coombs test is negative. Also give it at 28 weeks and after any sensitising event (abortion, ectopic, bleeding, amniocentesis). The Kleihauer-Betke test decides extra doses. Monitor an affected fetus with MCA Doppler.

Try a past question

A 34-year-old Rh-negative woman delivers an Rh- positive baby. Indirect coombs test is negative during pregnancy. By when should anti-D immunoglobulin be given postpartum for maximum effectiveness?

  1. A. Within 6 hours
  2. B. Within 72 hours
  3. C. Within 24 hours
  4. D. Within 7 days

Answer: B. Anti-D works best within 72 hours of delivery.

Preterm labor

High-yieldRepeated26 past questions

Preterm labour before 37 weeks: steroids (betamethasone) from 24 to 34 weeks for lung maturity, magnesium sulfate under 32 weeks for neuroprotection, tocolysis (nifedipine) only to buy time for steroids. Ruptured membranes are confirmed with nitrazine, fern or the more specific PAMG-1 test.

Try a past question

A 26-year-old woman with 34 weeks gestation presents with watery vaginal discharge. Pooling is seen on speculum examination. Which test is most specific for confirming rupture of membranes?

  1. A. Nitrazine test
  2. B. Fern test
  3. C. Placental alpha microglobulin-1(PAMG-1)test
  4. D. Ultrasound for amniotic fluid index

Answer: C. PAMG-1 is the most specific bedside test for rupture of membranes; nitrazine gives false positives.

Fetal medicine: aneuploidy screening, NIPT, fetal anomalies

AskedRepeatedINI-CET29 past questions

First-trimester combined test (11 to 14 weeks): nuchal translucency, PAPP-A and free β-hCG. Quadruple test in the second trimester. NIPT (cell-free DNA) is the best screen. Diagnosis needs CVS (10 to 13 weeks) or amniocentesis (from 15 weeks).

Try a past question

A 30-year-old woman has a 12-week pregnancy and wants screening for Down syndrome. Which is the best first-trimester combined screening?

  1. A. Nuchal translucency + PAPP-A + free beta-hCG
  2. B. AFP + ultrasound at 20 weeks
  3. C. Only CT scan
  4. D. Only glucose challenge test

Answer: A. Nuchal translucency with PAPP-A and free β-hCG is the first-trimester combined screen for Down syndrome.

Contraception

Must-doRepeated96 past questions

Combined pills are avoided in high blood pressure, migraine with aura, smokers over 35, breastfeeding under 6 weeks and past clots. Copper T 380A lasts 10 years and works as emergency contraception within 5 days. LNG-IUS reduces bleeding. Pearl index = failures per 100 woman-years.

Try a past question

A 32-year-old woman with a history of chronic hypertension seeks advice on contraception. She has no other significant medical issues. Which of the following contraceptive methods is contraindicated in her condition?

  1. A. CuT
  2. B. LNG-IUCD
  3. C. OCPs
  4. D. DMPA

Answer: C. The oestrogen in combined pills raises blood pressure and clot risk, so they are contraindicated in chronic hypertension.

Infertility

Must-doRepeated79 past questions

Work-up: semen analysis first for the man; ovulation by mid-luteal (day 21) progesterone; tubes by hysterosalpingography (or laparoscopy and dye, the gold standard). Ovulation induction: letrozole (first choice in PCOS) or clomiphene. Submucous fibroids that distort the cavity are removed when pregnancy is wanted.

Try a past question

A 30-year-old lady with primary infertility is being assessed for her ovulation. She has regular menstrual cycles. Day 3 - TVS shows multiple growing follicles. Serum level of which hormone can help you confirm ovulation?

  1. A. Progesterone
  2. B. Estrogen
  3. C. FSH
  4. D. LH

Answer: A. A raised mid-luteal progesterone confirms that ovulation happened.

PCOS

Must-doRepeated22 past questions

Rotterdam criteria, any 2 of 3: irregular or no ovulation, raised androgens (clinical or blood), polycystic ovaries on scan. Raised LH to FSH ratio, insulin resistance. Treatment: weight loss; pills for cycles and hirsutism; letrozole for fertility; metformin for insulin resistance.

Try a past question

A 30-year-old woman presents with oligomenorrhea, hirsutism, acne, and obesity. Laboratory studies show elevated LH with relatively normal FSH. What is the most likely diagnosis?

  1. A. Turner syndrome
  2. B. Polycystic ovary syndrome
  3. C. Sheehan syndrome
  4. D. Premature ovarian insufficiency

Answer: B. Oligomenorrhoea, hirsutism, acne and obesity with raised LH and normal FSH is polycystic ovary syndrome.

Abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB)

Must-doRepeated37 past questions

FIGO PALM-COEIN: structural (Polyp, Adenomyosis, Leiomyoma, Malignancy) or not (Coagulopathy, Ovulatory, Endometrial, Iatrogenic, Not classified). Postmenopausal bleeding is cancer until proven otherwise: scan, then biopsy or hysteroscopy. Hyperplasia without atypia: the LNG-IUS first.

Try a past question

A 46-year-old obese woman has abnormal uterine bleeding, and biopsy shows endometrial hyperplasia without atypia. She wishes to preserve her uterus. What is the best first-line long-term therapy?

  1. A. Copper IUCD
  2. B. Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system (LNG-IUS)
  3. C. Radiotherapy
  4. D. Chemotherapy

Answer: B. The LNG-IUS gives the endometrium a high local progestogen dose and is first-line for hyperplasia without atypia.

Fibroid uterus

Must-doRepeated23 past questions

The commonest benign tumour of the uterus. Submucous fibroids cause heavy bleeding and infertility; red degeneration happens in pregnancy (pain, treat conservatively). Wants fertility: myomectomy (hysteroscopic for submucous). Family complete: hysterectomy. GnRH analogues shrink them before surgery.

Try a past question

A 35-year-old woman presents with heavy menstrual bleeding. Ultrasonography reveals a 4- cm submucosal uterine fibroid distorting the endometrial cavity. She desires future pregnancy. Which treatment is most appropriate?

  1. A. Total abdominal hysterectomy
  2. B. Myomectomy
  3. C. Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
  4. D. Endometrial ablation

Answer: B. A submucous fibroid in a woman who wants to conceive is removed by myomectomy, which keeps the uterus.

Ectopic pregnancy

Must-doRepeated31 past questions

Commonest site: the ampulla of the tube. Pain, missed period, bleeding; shock if it ruptures (needs laparotomy). Medical treatment with methotrexate if stable, β-hCG under about 5,000, mass under 3.5 cm and no fetal heartbeat. Risk factors: PID, previous ectopic, tubal surgery, IUCD failure.

Try a past question

A 29-year-old woman has a 7-week ectopic pregnancy. She is stable, beta-hCG is 1800 mIU/mL, there is no fetal cardiac activity and the adnexal mass is 2.5 cm. What is the best management?

  1. A. Single-dose methotrexate
  2. B. Expectant management only
  3. C. Immediate laparotomy
  4. D. Hysterectomy

Answer: A. Stable, β-hCG 1,800, a small mass and no heartbeat: all fit single-dose methotrexate.

Pelvic organ prolapse

High-yieldRepeated28 past questions

Supports: the cardinal (Mackenrodt) and uterosacral ligaments. Graded by degree or by POP-Q. Young woman who wants children: a sling operation (it keeps the uterus). Fothergill (Manchester) when the family is complete but the uterus is to be kept. Elderly, unfit for surgery: Le Fort colpocleisis, or a pessary.

  3. C. lefort
  4. D. Shirodkar

Answer: A. A young nulliparous woman with prolapse needs a uterus-preserving sling operation, not Fothergill or Le Fort.

Endometriosis & adenomyosis

High-yieldRepeated48 past questions

Endometriosis: painful periods, deep dyspareunia, infertility, chocolate cysts, a fixed retroverted uterus and uterosacral nodules; diagnosed by laparoscopy. Adenomyosis: a uniformly enlarged, tender, boggy uterus in an older parous woman with heavy, painful periods.

  1. A. Adenomyosis
  2. B. Deep infiltrating endometriosis
  3. C. Chronic pelvic inflammatory disease
  4. D. Subserosal uterine fibroid

Answer: B. Deep dyspareunia with uterosacral nodules and a fixed retroverted uterus points to deep infiltrating endometriosis.

Menopause & HRT

High-yieldRepeated10 past questions

Menopause: no periods for 12 months, average age about 51. FSH and LH rise, oestrogen and progesterone fall; FSH is the best marker. Hormone therapy needs a progestogen added if the uterus is present (to protect the endometrium). Symptoms: hot flushes, night sweats, vaginal dryness.

Try a past question

A 52 years old postmenopausal woman presents with hot flushes and a dry vagina. What are the expected levels of FSH, LH, estrogen, and progesterone?

  1. A. ↑ LH, ↑ FSH, ↑ estrogen and progesterone
  2. B. ↓ LH, ↓ FSH, ↓ estrogen and progesterone
  3. C. ↑ LH, ↓ FSH, ↑ estrogen and progesterone
  4. D. ↑ LH, ↑ FSH, ↓ estrogen and progesterone

Answer: D. The failing ovary makes little oestrogen, so feedback is lost and FSH and LH rise.

Reproductive endocrinology: amenorrhea, hyperprolactinemia, premature ovarian failure

AskedRepeatedINI-CET81 past questions

Primary amenorrhoea: Müllerian agenesis (MRKH) has normal pubic hair; androgen insensitivity has breasts but scanty pubic hair, XY, and testes to remove after puberty; Turner has short stature with no breast development. Hyperprolactinaemia: cabergoline first. Sheehan: pituitary failure after a big PPH.

Try a past question

A 14-year-old girl presents with primary amenorrhea. On examination, there are well developed breasts , the uterus and ovaries are absent with a blind ending vagina. Pubic hair is scanty. What is the most likely diagnosis?

  1. A. Turner syndrome
  2. B. Androgen insensitivity syndrome
  3. C. Gonadal dysgenesis
  4. D. Müllerian agenesis

Answer: B. Breasts but scanty pubic hair, a blind vagina and no uterus is androgen insensitivity (XY), not Müllerian agenesis.

Gestational trophoblastic disease (molar pregnancy)

High-yieldRepeated41 past questions

Gestational trophoblastic disease: complete mole (46XX, no fetus, very high hCG), partial mole (triploid). Treatment is suction evacuation, then follow hCG to normal. Persistent or rising hCG means trophoblastic neoplasia: methotrexate.

  1. A. Suction and evacuation
  2. B. Dilatation and curettage
  3. C. Hysterectomy
  4. D. Methotrexate therapy

Answer: A. A complete mole is emptied by suction evacuation; follow-up with hCG comes next.

Cervical cancer: HPV, screening, staging

High-yieldRepeated62 past questions

HPV 16 and 18 cause most cervical cancers. India screens women 30 to 65 with VIA (visual inspection with acetic acid). HPV vaccine before sexual activity. Staging is FIGO. Early stage (IB1) is treated by radical (type III) hysterectomy; advanced stages by chemoradiation.

Try a past question

A 32-year-old woman presents for routine cervical cancer screening. Her HPV test is positive for a high- risk genotype associated with cervical carcinoma. Which HPV types are most strongly associated with cervical cancer?

  1. A. HPV 31 and 32
  2. B. HPV 16 and 18
  3. C. HPV 6 and 11
  4. D. HPV 1 and 2

Answer: B. High-risk HPV types 16 and 18 are linked to most cervical cancers; 6 and 11 cause warts.

Endometrial cancer

High-yieldRepeated28 past questions

Risk: unopposed oestrogen, obesity, diabetes, hypertension, nulliparity, PCOS, tamoxifen, Lynch syndrome. Presents as postmenopausal bleeding; an endometrial thickness over 4 mm needs a biopsy. Staging is FIGO surgical staging.

Try a past question

  1. A. Duke staging
  2. B. Ann Arbor staging
  3. C. TNM clinical staging only
  4. D. FIGO surgical staging

Answer: D. Endometrial cancer is staged surgically with the FIGO system.

Ovarian tumors and tumor markers

High-yieldRepeated34 past questions

Know the tumour markers: CA-125 (epithelial), AFP (yolk sac, Schiller-Duval bodies), β-hCG (choriocarcinoma), LDH (dysgerminoma), inhibin (granulosa cell). Mature cystic teratoma (dermoid) is the commonest germ cell tumour. Meigs syndrome: fibroma with ascites and pleural effusion.

Try a past question

A 19-year-old woman presents with a rapidly enlarging ovarian mass. Histopathology shows a glomerulus-like structure containing a central vessel surrounded by tumor cells. Which tumor marker is most likely to be elevated?

  1. A. CA-125
  2. B. Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)
  3. C. Inhibin B
  4. D. β-hCG exclusively

Answer: B. Glomerulus-like Schiller-Duval bodies mean a yolk sac tumour, which makes alpha-fetoprotein.

Vulvar cancer

High-yieldRepeated7 past questions

Mostly squamous cell carcinoma in older women, often after lichen sclerosus or VIN. Itching and a lump or ulcer; it spreads first to the inguinal nodes. Staging is FIGO; treated by wide local excision or radical vulvectomy with node surgery.
