Carbohydrate Metabolism 124 questions

Glycolysis

Must-do22 past questions

Phosphofructokinase-1 is rate-limiting, activated by fructose-2,6-bisphosphate and AMP, inhibited by ATP and citrate. Net gain: 2 ATP per glucose in anaerobic glycolysis. Red cells depend fully on glycolysis; pyruvate kinase deficiency causes haemolysis.

Try a past question

Which enzyme catalyzes the rate-limiting step of glycolysis and is allosterically activated by fructose-2,6-bisphosphate?

  1. A. Hexokinase
  2. B. Pyruvate kinase
  3. C. Glucokinase
  4. D. Phosphofructokinase-1

Answer: D. PFK-1 is the rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis and is activated by fructose-2,6-bisphosphate.

TCA cycle

Must-do22 past questions

Takes place in the mitochondrial matrix. Isocitrate dehydrogenase is the rate-limiting enzyme. One turn gives 3 NADH, 1 FADH2, 1 GTP. Pyruvate dehydrogenase links glycolysis to the cycle (needs B1, B2, B3, B5 and lipoic acid; arsenic blocks it).

Gluconeogenesis

Must-do14 past questions

Makes glucose from lactate, glycerol and amino acids, mainly in the liver (and kidney in starvation). Key enzymes: pyruvate carboxylase (activated by acetyl-CoA, needs biotin), PEPCK, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, glucose-6-phosphatase. Muscle lacks glucose-6-phosphatase.

Try a past question

Which metabolite activates pyruvate carboxylase during gluconeogenesis?

  1. A. Citrate
  2. B. NADH
  3. C. Acetyl-CoA
  4. D. ATP

Answer: C. Acetyl-CoA activates pyruvate carboxylase, pushing pyruvate towards glucose.

HMP shunt

Must-do20 past questions

The pentose phosphate pathway makes NADPH (for fat synthesis and to keep glutathione reduced) and ribose-5-phosphate (for nucleotides). G6PD is the rate-limiting enzyme; its deficiency leaves red cells open to oxidant damage (Heinz bodies, bite cells). Transketolase needs thiamine.

Try a past question

The pentose phosphate pathway primarily generates which two important products?

  1. A. ATP and NADH
  2. B. FADHz and Pyruvate
  3. C. Lactate and ATP
  4. D. NADPH and Ribose-5-phosphate

Answer: D. The pentose phosphate pathway produces NADPH and ribose-5-phosphate, not ATP.

Glycogen metabolism and storage disorders

Must-doRepeated37 past questions

Glycogen storage diseases: type I Von Gierke (glucose-6-phosphatase): fasting hypoglycaemia, lactic acidosis, high uric acid and lipids, big liver. II Pompe (acid maltase, lysosomal): heart failure in infants. III Cori (debranching). V McArdle (muscle phosphorylase): cramps on exercise, no lactate rise. Glycogen synthase and phosphorylase are the key enzymes.

Molecular Biology & Genetics 107 questions

DNA replication and repair

Must-do34 past questions

DNA polymerase III makes most new DNA in bacteria; telomerase keeps chromosome ends. Repair: nucleotide excision (thymine dimers from UV; xeroderma pigmentosum), base excision, mismatch repair (Lynch syndrome), homologous recombination (BRCA).

Try a past question

  3. C. Mismatch repair
  4. D. Homologous recombination

Answer: A. Thymine dimers from UV light are removed by nucleotide excision repair.

Transcription and RNA processing

Must-do18 past questions

RNA polymerase I: rRNA; II: mRNA (blocked by α-amanitin); III: tRNA and 5S rRNA. mRNA processing: 5′ cap, poly-A tail, splicing of introns (snRNPs; anti-Smith antibodies target them).

Try a past question

Which RNA polymerase synthesizes mRNA in eukaryotic cells?

  1. A. RNA polymerase I
  2. B. RNA polymerase II
  3. C. RNA polymerase III
  4. D. Reverse transcriptase

Answer: B. RNA polymerase II makes messenger RNA in eukaryotes.

Protein synthesis

Must-do15 past questions

Translation starts at AUG (methionine); stop codons UAA, UAG, UGA. The genetic code is degenerate and universal. Post-translational changes: hydroxylation (collagen, needs vitamin C), glycosylation, phosphorylation, gamma-carboxylation (vitamin K), subunit assembly. Proteins are tagged for breakdown with ubiquitin.

Genetics: chromosomal disorders, imprinting, inheritance patterns

Must-do34 past questions

Autosomal dominant: often structural (Marfan, ADPKD, Huntington, NF). Autosomal recessive: mostly enzyme defects. X-linked recessive: haemophilia, Duchenne (dystrophin, the largest gene), G6PD, colour blindness. Mitochondrial: from the mother only. Imprinting: Prader-Willi (paternal 15q loss), Angelman (maternal).

Answer: A. Duchenne muscular dystrophy is caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene.

Fragile X, Prader-Willi, Angelman, Huntington, myotonic dystrophy

Must-doRepeated7 past questions

Trinucleotide repeats: Fragile X (CGG; long face, big ears, large testes), Huntington (CAG), myotonic dystrophy (CTG), Friedreich ataxia (GAA). Anticipation: worse and earlier in later generations. Cystic fibrosis: CFTR chloride channel (ΔF508 the commonest).

  3. C. Ca²⁺ absorption
  4. D. K+ secretion

Answer: B. The CFTR protein is a chloride channel; its defect blocks chloride transport.

Amino Acid Metabolism 94 questions

Urea cycle

Must-do23 past questions

Happens in the liver, partly in mitochondria. Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I is rate-limiting, activated by N-acetylglutamate. Ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency (X-linked) is the commonest urea cycle defect: high ammonia and orotic acid. High ammonia without acidosis suggests a urea cycle defect.

Try a past question

Which compound is the allosteric activator of carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I in the urea cycle?

  1. A. N-acetylglutamate
  2. B. Arginine
  3. C. Citrulline
  4. D. Aspartate

Answer: A. N-acetylglutamate is the essential allosteric activator of carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I.

PKU, alkaptonuria, MSUD, homocystinuria, tyrosinemia

Must-do50 past questions

PKU: phenylalanine hydroxylase, musty odour, fair skin. Alkaptonuria: homogentisate oxidase, urine darkens on standing, blue-black ear cartilage (ochronosis), arthritis. MSUD: branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase. Homocystinuria: cystathionine β-synthase, downward lens dislocation, clots. Albinism: tyrosinase.

  4. D. Fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase deficiency

Answer: B. Dark urine with blue-black ear cartilage is alkaptonuria, from homogentisate oxidase deficiency.

Neurotransmitters

Must-do12 past questions

Lipid Metabolism 82 questions

β-oxidation, carnitine shuttle, fatty acid synthesis, ketone bodies

Must-do30 past questions

Carnitine carries long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria (carnitine palmitoyltransferase I, blocked by malonyl-CoA). β-oxidation gives acetyl-CoA. Fatty acid synthesis: acetyl-CoA carboxylase is rate-limiting (needs biotin). Ketone bodies (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate) are made in the liver but the liver cannot use them.

Try a past question

  1. A. Biotin
  2. B. Carnitine
  3. C. Coenzyme Q
  4. D. Lipoic acid

Answer: B. Carnitine shuttles long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

Lipoproteins

Must-do34 past questions

Chylomicrons: dietary fat (apoB-48). VLDL: liver-made triglyceride. LDL: carries cholesterol to tissues (apoB-100); familial hypercholesterolaemia is an LDL receptor defect. HDL: reverse cholesterol transport (apoA-I). Lipoprotein lipase needs apoC-II.

Try a past question

Which lipoprotein is primarily responsible for reverse cholesterol transport?

  1. A. LDL
  2. B. VLDL
  3. C. HDL
  4. D. Chylomicron

Answer: C. HDL carries cholesterol from tissues back to the liver: reverse cholesterol transport.

Cholesterol

Must-do14 past questions

HMG-CoA reductase is the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis (statins block it). Cholesterol makes bile acids, steroid hormones and vitamin D. HDL takes cholesterol back to the liver.

Enzymes 48 questions

Enzyme kinetics and inhibition

Must-do22 past questions

Km = substrate concentration at half Vmax (low Km = high affinity). Turnover number (kcat): molecules converted per enzyme per unit time. Competitive inhibitor: Km up, Vmax same. Non-competitive: Vmax down, Km same. Lineweaver-Burk plot shows the difference.

Try a past question

An enzyme converts 600 substrate molecules into product per minute when fully saturated with substrate. What is this parameter called?

  1. A. Km
  2. B. Turnover number (Kcat)
  3. C. Vmax
  4. D. Specific activity

Answer: B. Molecules converted per minute by a fully saturated enzyme is the turnover number (kcat).

Clinical enzymes

Must-do15 past questions

ALT: the most specific for liver cell injury. ALP and GGT: cholestasis (ALP also bone). Amylase and lipase: pancreatitis (lipase more specific, stays up longer). Troponin: heart. CK-MB rises again with re-infarction. LDH rises in haemolysis and lymphoma.

  4. D. Alkaline phosphatase

Answer: C. Amylase (and lipase) rise in acute pancreatitis.

Vitamins & Cofactors 42 questions

Vitamins and cofactors

Must-do42 past questions

B1 thiamine (pyruvate dehydrogenase, transketolase): beriberi, Wernicke. B2: cheilosis. B3 niacin: pellagra (dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia), also in Hartnup and carcinoid. B6: neuropathy with isoniazid, sideroblastic anaemia. B12 and folate: megaloblastic anaemia. Vitamin C (with copper) is needed for collagen cross-linking: scurvy.

Try a past question

Which deficiency leads to collagenation defect ?

  1. A. Ascorbic acid and copper.
  2. B. Zinc and copper
  3. C. Ascorbic acid and iron
  4. D. iodine and iron

Answer: A. Collagen needs vitamin C for hydroxylation and copper (lysyl oxidase) for cross-linking.

Inborn Errors of Metabolism 69 questions

Inborn errors of metabolism (overview)

High-yield69 past questions

Most are autosomal recessive. Clues: a sick newborn after feeds starting, unusual urine smell, hypoglycaemia, acidosis or high ammonia. Galactosaemia (cataract, E. coli sepsis), PKU (musty odour), MSUD (maple syrup urine), urea cycle defects (high ammonia, no acidosis), Von Gierke (hypoglycaemia, lactic acidosis).

Try a past question

  1. A. Debranching enzyme
  2. B. Glucose-6-phosphatase
  3. C. Muscle phosphorylase
  4. D. Pyruvate kinase

Answer: B. Fasting hypoglycaemia, lactic acidosis, high uric acid and a big liver point to glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency (Von Gierke).

Cell Biology & Organelles 52 questions

Organelles and their diseases

High-yieldINI-CET37 past questions

Peroxisomes: very long chain fatty acid breakdown (Zellweger syndrome, adrenoleukodystrophy). Lysosomes: storage diseases (Tay-Sachs, Gaucher, Niemann-Pick, Pompe); I-cell disease is a failure to tag enzymes with mannose-6-phosphate. Mitochondria: energy, inherited from the mother.

Try a past question

Zellweger syndrome is due to which of the following ?

  1. A. Nucleosomes
  2. B. Peroxisomes
  3. C. Lysosomes
  4. D. Ribosomes

Answer: B. Zellweger syndrome is a failure to form peroxisomes.

Cytoskeleton

High-yieldINI-CET6 past questions

Microtubules (tubulin): kinesin moves cargo outward (anterograde), dynein moves it back towards the cell body (retrograde; rabies and polio travel this way). Defective dynein: Kartagener syndrome (sinusitis, bronchiectasis, situs inversus, infertility). Colchicine and vincristine act on microtubules.

Try a past question

  1. A. Kinesin
  2. B. Dynein
  3. C. Myosin
  4. D. Actin

Answer: B. Dynein carries cargo back towards the cell body, the route rabies virus uses to reach the brain.

Heme Metabolism 39 questions

Heme synthesis, lead poisoning, porphyrias

High-yieldINI-CET21 past questions

ALA synthase is rate-limiting (needs B6). Lead blocks ALA dehydratase and ferrochelatase: basophilic stippling, microcytic anaemia. Acute intermittent porphyria (PBG deaminase): belly pain, neuropathy, psychiatric signs, no skin photosensitivity; drugs and fasting trigger attacks. Porphyria cutanea tarda: blisters on sun-exposed skin.

Try a past question

Which enzyme catalyzes the rate-limiting step of heme synthesis?

  1. A. Ferrochelatase
  2. B. Porphobilinogen deaminase
  3. C. ALA dehydratase
  4. D. ALA synthase

Answer: D. ALA synthase is the rate-limiting enzyme of haem synthesis.

Bilirubin metabolism

High-yieldINI-CET16 past questions

Haem → biliverdin → unconjugated bilirubin → conjugated in the liver by UDP-glucuronyl transferase. Unconjugated rises: haemolysis, Gilbert (mild, after fasting or stress), Crigler-Najjar, newborn jaundice (immature enzyme). Conjugated rises: Dubin-Johnson (black liver), Rotor, obstruction.

Try a past question

A newborn develops jaundice on the second day of life. Laboratory findings show elevated unconjugated bilirubin with normal liver enzymes. Which enzyme immaturity is responsible?

  1. A. Heme oxygenase
  2. B. Biliverdin reductase
  3. C. UDP-glucuronyl transferase
  4. D. Alanine aminotransferase

Answer: C. Newborns have immature UDP-glucuronyl transferase, so unconjugated bilirubin builds up.

Clinical Biochemistry 18 questions

Tumor markers

High-yieldRepeatedINI-CET10 past questions

AFP: liver cancer and yolk sac tumours. CEA: colorectal. CA-125: ovary. CA 19-9: pancreas. PSA: prostate. β-hCG: choriocarcinoma, molar pregnancy. Calcitonin: medullary thyroid. Used for follow-up rather than screening (PSA aside).

  3. C. AFP
  4. D. CEA

Answer: C. AFP is the marker most often raised in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Nutrition 17 questions

Nutrition: protein-energy malnutrition and energy

Asked17 past questions

Kwashiorkor: oedema, fatty liver, flaky paint skin, sparse pale hair, low albumin (protein lack). Marasmus: severe wasting without oedema (energy lack). Energy: carbohydrate and protein 4 kcal/g, fat 9 kcal/g, alcohol 7. Essential fatty acids: linoleic and linolenic.

Try a past question

  1. A. Marasmus
  2. B. Kwashiorkor
  3. C. Vitamin D deficiency
  4. D. Rickets

Answer: B. Oedema, a swollen belly, sparse discoloured hair and skin depigmentation are kwashiorkor.
