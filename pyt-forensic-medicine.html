Forensic Toxicology 98 questions

Organophosphorus (OP poisoning)

Must-doRepeated14 past questions

Organophosphates: pinpoint pupils, salivation, sweating, bronchorrhoea, slow pulse, fasciculations; a kerosene-like smell. Atropine titrated to dry secretions, and pralidoxime early. Commonest poisoning in rural India.

Try a past question

A patient presents with excessive salivation, pinpoint pupils, sweating, and muscle fasciculations after pesticide exposure. The most likely poisoning is?

  1. A. Organophosphorus compound
  2. B. Atropine poisoning
  3. C. Carbon monoxide poisoning
  4. D. Cyanide poisoning

Answer: A. Salivation, pinpoint pupils, sweating and fasciculations after pesticide exposure is organophosphate poisoning.

Datura (anticholinergic toxidrome)

Must-doRepeated7 past questions

Datura (anticholinergic): dry as a bone, red as a beet, hot as a hare, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter. Dilated pupils, dry hot skin, delirium, urinary retention. Used in road robbery poisonings. Antidote: physostigmine.

Carbon monoxide

Must-doRepeated17 past questions

Carbon monoxide: closed rooms with a stove or a running engine. Cherry-red skin, blood and organs; carboxyhaemoglobin. Treat with 100% oxygen, hyperbaric oxygen if severe. Late brain damage (delayed neuropsychiatric syndrome) can follow.

  3. C. Hydrogen sulphide poisoning
  4. D. Organophosphate poisoning

Answer: B. A closed garage with an engine running and cherry-red blood is carbon monoxide poisoning.

Cyanide

Must-doRepeated7 past questions

Cyanide blocks cytochrome c oxidase, so cells cannot use oxygen: bitter almond smell, bright red blood. Treatment: hydroxocobalamin, or nitrites to form methaemoglobin, then sodium thiosulfate.

Try a past question

Which enzyme is directly inhibited by cyanide poisoning?

  1. A. NADH dehydrogenase
  2. B. Cytochrome c oxidase
  3. C. Succinate dehydrogenase
  4. D. ATP synthase

Answer: B. Cyanide inhibits cytochrome c oxidase (complex IV), stopping cellular respiration.

Snake bite

Must-doRepeated6 past questions

Viper (Russell's, saw-scaled): local swelling, bleeding, kidney failure; test clotting with the 20-minute whole blood clotting test. Cobra and krait (neurotoxic): ptosis, paralysis, breathing failure. Polyvalent anti-snake venom; no tourniquet or incision.

Alcohol intoxication

Must-doRepeated12 past questions

Blood alcohol is the best evidence of drunkenness; preserve blood with sodium fluoride. Wernicke: confusion, ataxia, eye-movement palsy; give thiamine. Korsakoff: memory loss with confabulation. Delirium tremens: 2-4 days after stopping, with tremor and hallucinations.

  3. C. Korsakoff psychosis
  4. D. Acute alcohol intoxication

Answer: B. Confusion, ataxia and ophthalmoplegia in a chronic alcoholic is Wernicke's encephalopathy.

Cocaine

Must-doRepeated11 past questions

Cocaine: dilated pupils, high BP, fast pulse, chest pain, arrhythmias; cocaine bugs (formication: feeling insects under the skin, so scratch marks). Treat agitation with benzodiazepines; avoid β-blockers alone.

Try a past question

  1. A. Heroin
  2. B. Cocaine
  3. C. Datura
  4. D. Cannabis

Answer: B. Formication (cocaine bugs) causes scratch marks; with tachycardia and arrhythmias it points to cocaine.

Medical Law & Ethics 71 questions

Consent (informed, implied, proxy)

Must-do15 past questions

A person aged 12 or more can consent to a physical examination; 18 or more for treatment or surgery. A sexual assault victim aged 12 or more consents to her own examination; under 12, a parent or guardian consents. Implied consent covers routine examination; a life-saving emergency may proceed without consent. Informed consent explains risks and alternatives.

Try a past question

  1. A. Police officer
  2. B. Parents/guardian
  3. C. The victim herself
  4. D. Magistrate

Answer: C. A 16-year-old victim can give consent for her own medical examination.

Medical negligence

Must-do10 past questions

Negligence needs four Ds: duty, dereliction (breach), direct causation, damage. Res ipsa loquitur ('the thing speaks for itself'): a swab left inside, operating on the wrong side. Contributory negligence: the patient also at fault. Criminal negligence: BNS 106 (causing death by negligence).

BNS sections

Must-do19 past questions

BNS replaced the IPC from 1 July 2024. Key sections: murder 103, culpable homicide 100-101, death by negligence 106, dowry death 80, rape 63 (defined) and 64 (punishment), rape causing death or a vegetative state 66. BNSS replaced CrPC (police inquest: BNSS 194); BSA replaced the Evidence Act.

POCSO Act

Must-do8 past questions

POCSO Act 2012 covers anyone under 18. Every doctor must report suspected sexual abuse of a child (failing is an offence). A female doctor should examine a girl; a parent or trusted adult present. Free medical care; no need to wait for police.

Dying declaration vs dying deposition

Must-do4 past questions

Dying declaration: a statement by a dying person about the cause of death; can be recorded by anyone (best a magistrate), no oath, no cross-examination, admissible under the evidence law (BSA 26, formerly IEA 32). Dying deposition: recorded by a magistrate with the accused present; stronger, rarely used.

Injuries 48 questions

Abrasion types

Must-do5 past questions

Abrasions involve only the epidermis and heal without scarring. Types: scratch, graze (shows direction of force), pressure abrasion (ligature mark), and patterned (imprint) abrasion that copies the object's surface, like a tyre tread.

Answer: B. A patterned abrasion reproduces the surface pattern of the object that caused it.

Bruise aging

Must-do8 past questions

Colour changes over time: red at first, blue to purple about 1-3 days, green about 5-7 days, yellow about 7-10 days, gone by 2 weeks. Bruises can move under gravity (ectopic bruising). Ageing bruises by colour is only approximate.

  3. C. 4-5 days
  4. D. 7-10 days

Answer: B. A bluish-purple bruise is roughly 1-3 days old.

Incised vs lacerated vs stab wounds

Must-do24 past questions

Incised wound: clean edges, longer than deep. Laceration: torn edges, tissue bridges, from blunt force. Stab: deeper than long. Hesitation cuts: several shallow parallel cuts on the wrist of the non-dominant side, typical of suicide. Defence wounds on the forearms and palms suggest homicide.

  2. B. Suicidal Injury with hesitational cuts
  3. C. Accidental injury
  4. D. Defense wounds

Answer: B. Multiple shallow parallel cuts on the non-dominant wrist are hesitation cuts of a suicide attempt.

Asphyxia 18 questions

Hanging vs strangulation vs throttling

Must-doRepeated13 past questions

Hanging: ligature mark oblique, incomplete, above the thyroid cartilage, with a dribble of saliva; usually suicidal. Ligature strangulation: mark horizontal, complete, low on the neck; usually homicidal. Throttling (manual strangulation): fingernail marks and bruises, hyoid fracture. Amussat sign (a transverse tear in the carotid intima) is seen in hanging.

Try a past question

Amussat's sign is most commonly seen in which condition?

  1. A. Manual strangulation
  2. B. Drowning
  3. C. Traumatic asphyxia
  4. D. Hanging

Answer: D. Amussat's sign, a tear in the carotid intima, is classically seen in hanging.

Ligature mark features

Must-doRepeated5 past questions

A horizontal, complete (encircling) mark below the thyroid cartilage, with uniform depth, means ligature strangulation. An oblique mark rising to a knot, not complete, above the thyroid cartilage, means hanging. The base of a hanging mark is parchment-like.

Try a past question

  1. A. Suicidal hanging
  2. B. Manual strangulation
  3. C. Ligature strangulation
  4. D. Traumatic asphyxia

Answer: C. A horizontal mark that fully circles the neck low down is ligature strangulation.

Petechial haemorrhages

Must-doRepeated3 past questions

Tardieu spots: small petechial haemorrhages under the pleura, pericardium and in the eyes, typical of asphyxial deaths. Seen most in strangulation and traumatic asphyxia. Not specific on their own.

Postmortem Changes 27 questions

Rigor mortis

High-yield9 past questions

Rigor mortis starts in about 1-2 hours, first in the heart and then the small muscles of the eyelids and jaw (masseter), spreads down the body (Nysten law), fully established by about 12 hours, lasts about 12 hours more. Cadaveric spasm: instant stiffening at death (a weapon gripped in the hand), cannot be faked.

Try a past question

Which of the following is the earliest sign of rigor mortis?

  1. A. Neck muscles
  2. B. Lower limb muscles
  3. C. Masseter muscles
  4. D. Upper limb muscles

Answer: C. Rigor appears first in small muscles such as the masseter (after the heart).

Livor mortis

High-yield8 past questions

Post-mortem staining (hypostasis) begins in 1-2 hours, fixed by about 6-8 hours. Its colour hints at the cause: cherry red (CO, cyanide), chocolate brown (nitrites, methaemoglobin), bright red (cold, hypothermia). Contact pressure areas stay pale.

Time since death estimation

High-yield9 past questions

Earliest change after death: the body starts cooling (algor mortis) and the eyes change (cornea clouds, retinal vessel 'trucking'). Order: cooling and eye changes, then staining, then rigor, then putrefaction (green colour first over the right iliac fossa), then adipocere or mummification. Vitreous potassium rises steadily after death.

Try a past question

  2. B. Algor mortis
  3. C. Putrefaction
  4. D. Adipocere formation

Answer: B. Cooling (algor mortis) starts as soon as circulation stops, before rigor or putrefaction.

Sexual Offences 15 questions

Rape definition

High-yield8 past questions

Rape is defined in BNS 63 and punished under 64; rape causing death or a persistent vegetative state is BNS 66. Examination needs consent, a female attendant and evidence collection (swabs, clothing) as early as possible; two-finger test is banned.

Identification 35 questions

Age estimation

Asked20 past questions

Children: teeth eruption (temporary teeth start at 6 months, complete by about 2.5 years; permanent from 6 years) and ossification centres. Adults: Gustafson's method (six dental changes) and skull suture closure; pubic symphysis changes. X-ray of the wrist and elbow in teens.

Try a past question

Which test is used for age estimation in adults based on dental changes?

  1. A. Demirjian method
  2. B. Tanner staging
  3. C. Gustafson method
  4. D. Ossification center assessment

Answer: C. Gustafson's method estimates adult age from regressive changes in teeth.

Sex determination

Asked4 past questions

The pelvis is the best bone for sex; the skull is next. Male pelvis: narrow greater sciatic notch, subpubic angle under 70°, heart-shaped inlet. Female: wide notch, subpubic angle over 90°. Male skull: heavier, prominent brow ridges and mastoids, square chin.

Autopsy 12 questions

Viscera preservation

AskedRepeated8 past questions

Common preservative for viscera: saturated sodium chloride solution. Rectified spirit is used except in alcohol, kerosene, phenol and some other poisonings. Blood for alcohol: sodium fluoride (with potassium oxalate). Formalin is for histology, never for chemical analysis.

Try a past question

In a case of alcohol abuse, blood during autopsy is preserved in

  1. A. Sodium fluoride
  2. B. Rectified spirit
  3. C. Sodium chloride
  4. D. Formalin

Answer: A. Sodium fluoride stops fermentation and keeps the blood alcohol level from changing.

Documents & Certificates 5 questions

Documents and certificates

Asked5 past questions

Medical certificates must be true and given only after examination. Death certificate: cause in sequence (immediate, then underlying cause last line in part I). A doctor can refuse to issue a death certificate when foul play is suspected; inform police.
