Antimicrobials 141 questions

Anti-TB drugs

Must-doRepeated33 past questions

Know each first-line drug's signature adverse effect: isoniazid neuropathy (give pyridoxine) and hepatitis, rifampicin orange secretions and enzyme induction, pyrazinamide hyperuricaemia and hepatitis, ethambutol optic neuritis (red-green colour vision). New drug-sensitive TB: 2 months HRZE, then 4 months HRE.

Try a past question

A 35-year-old man presents with fever, cough, night sweats, and weight loss. Chest radiograph shows upper-lobe cavitary lesions, and sputum examination is positive for acid-fast bacilli. Which treatment regimen is appropriate for drug-sensitive disease?

  1. A. Rifampicin alone
  2. B. Isoniazid + rifampicin + pyrazinamide + ethambutol
  3. C. Ethambutol alone
  4. D. Isoniazid + streptomycin alone

Answer: B. Drug-sensitive pulmonary TB starts with four drugs, isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol, in the intensive phase.

Penicillins, cephalosporins, macrolides, aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones

Must-doRepeated74 past questions

High-yield pairs: MSSA, cloxacillin; MRSA, vancomycin; SBP, cefotaxime; aminoglycosides, nephro- and ototoxicity; fluoroquinolones, tendon rupture; macrolides, QT prolongation; metronidazole with alcohol, a disulfiram-like reaction.

Try a past question

A 12-year-old child presents with fever, knee pain, and swelling. Synovial fluid culture grows a beta-hemolytic, Gram-positive coccus arranged in clusters that is catalase-positive and sensitive to oxacillin. What is the drug of choice?

  1. A. Vancomycin
  2. B. Cloxacillin
  3. C. Azithromycin
  4. D. Penicillin

Answer: B. Oxacillin-sensitive Staph aureus is MSSA, and a penicillinase-resistant penicillin (cloxacillin) is the drug of choice.

Antiretroviral therapy (ART)

Must-doRepeated18 past questions

Tenofovir: renal toxicity (Fanconi syndrome) and bone loss. Zidovudine: anaemia. Abacavir: hypersensitivity (HLA-B*5701). Efavirenz: vivid dreams. Tenofovir and lamivudine also treat hepatitis B.

Try a past question

A patient with HIV is being treated with a regimen including Tenofovir. Which of the following is a characteristic adverse effect associated with this drug?

  1. A. Fanconi Syndrome
  2. B. Pancreatitis
  3. C. Peripheral Neuropathy
  4. D. Megaloblastic Anemia

Answer: A. Tenofovir damages the proximal tubule, giving Fanconi syndrome: glycosuria, phosphaturia and aminoaciduria.

Autonomic Nervous System 133 questions

Cholinergic and anticholinergic drugs

Must-doRepeated30 past questions

Neostigmine reverses non-depolarising block and does not cross into the brain; physostigmine does (atropine poisoning). Atropine is first-line for symptomatic bradycardia. Anticholinergic toxicity: hot, dry, red, blind, mad.

Try a past question

Which drug is used for reversal of non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockade after surgery?

  1. A. Neostigmine
  2. B. Succinylcholine
  3. C. Atropine
  4. D. Rocuronium

Answer: A. Neostigmine raises acetylcholine at the motor end plate, which out-competes the non-depolarising blocker.

Adrenergic drugs

Must-doRepeated35 past questions

α1 = vasoconstriction (why adrenaline is added to local anaesthetics), β1 = heart, β2 = bronchodilation. Adrenaline is the drug of choice in anaphylaxis (IM). Tamsulosin (α1A-selective) for BPH causes first-dose postural hypotension.

Try a past question

Adrenaline is added along with local anesthesia, so that there should be less bleeding on local surgical spot. This action of adrenaline is produced by the stimulation of which receptor?

  1. A. α1 receptor
  2. B. α2 receptor
  3. C. β2 receptor
  4. D. β3 receptor

Answer: A. Adrenaline constricts local vessels through α1 receptors, so less bleeding and slower washout of the anaesthetic.

Beta-blockers

Must-doRepeated24 past questions

Non-selective β-blockers (propranolol, timolol) are avoided in asthma. Propranolol is first-line for migraine prophylaxis, performance anxiety and thyrotoxic symptoms. β-blocker overdose: give glucagon, which raises cAMP without β receptors.

Try a past question

A patient is brought to the ED with severe bradycardia, hypotension and wheezing after an intentional overdose of propranolol. Which is the preferred antidote?

  1. A. Atropine
  2. B. Glucagon
  3. C. Calcium gluconate
  4. D. Digoxin-specific Fab

Answer: B. Glucagon acts on its own receptor to raise cAMP in the heart, bypassing the blocked β receptors.

Organophosphate poisoning

Must-doRepeated15 past questions

Organophosphates block acetylcholinesterase irreversibly: muscarinic signs (salivation, sweating, bronchorrhoea, miosis, bradycardia) plus nicotinic weakness. Atropine is given first and titrated to dry secretions; pralidoxime reactivates the enzyme only before it 'ages'.

Try a past question

A person is exposed to a poison that inhibits acetylcholinesterase. Which substance is used as the principal antidotal therapy for muscarinic manifestations?

  1. A. Naloxone
  2. B. Atropine
  3. C. Flumazenil
  4. D. Deferoxamine

Answer: B. Atropine blocks the muscarinic receptors flooded with acetylcholine; it does not help the nicotinic weakness.

Myasthenia gravis

Must-doRepeated10 past questions

Antibodies against nicotinic ACh receptors: ptosis and fatigable weakness that worsens through the day. Pyridostigmine is the mainstay for symptoms; edrophonium (Tensilon) was the classic diagnostic test. Watch aminoglycosides, which worsen myasthenia.

Try a past question

Which drug is used in the treatment of myasthenia gravis and increases acetylcholine levels at the neuromuscular junction?

  1. A. Pyridostigmine
  2. B. Atropine
  3. C. Rocuronium
  4. D. Botulinum toxin

Answer: A. Pyridostigmine inhibits acetylcholinesterase, so more acetylcholine reaches the reduced number of receptors.

Glaucoma drugs

Must-doRepeated16 past questions

Prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost) are first-line for open-angle glaucoma: more uveoscleral outflow, once daily, darkens the iris. Timolol reduces aqueous production but can trigger asthma by systemic absorption. Pilocarpine and acetazolamide for acute angle closure.

Try a past question

After treatment for glaucoma, a patient comes to emergency department with acute bronchial asthma. Which drug might have caused this?

  1. A. Latanoprost
  2. B. Timolol
  3. C. Betaxolol
  4. D. Anticholinesterase

Answer: B. Timolol is a non-selective β-blocker; drops absorbed through the nasolacrimal duct block β2 receptors in the lungs.

Pheochromocytoma management

Must-doRepeated8 past questions

Alpha-blockade first (phenoxybenzamine), beta-blocker only afterwards. A β-blocker alone leaves α vasoconstriction unopposed and can cause a hypertensive crisis. Diagnosis: plasma free or urinary metanephrines.

Try a past question

A 39-year-old woman presents with episodic headache, palpitations, sweating, and paroxysmal hypertension. Plasma free metanephrines are markedly elevated, and imaging identifies an adrenal mass. Which preoperative management is most appropriate?

  1. A. Start propranolol first and add phenoxybenzamine later
  2. B. Begin alpha-adrenergic blockade, then add beta-blockade if needed after adequate alpha blockade
  3. C. Administer a beta-blocker alone to prevent tachycardia
  4. D. Proceed directly to surgery without blood pressure optimization

Answer: B. Alpha first, then beta: unopposed α stimulation is the danger of starting with a β-blocker.

CVS Pharmacology 131 questions

Hypertension

Must-do65 past questions

ACE inhibitors: dry cough and angioedema (bradykinin), avoid in pregnancy. Pregnancy: labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa. Hypertensive emergency: lower the pressure gradually with IV drugs such as labetalol, nicardipine or nitroprusside. Never combine an ACE inhibitor with sacubitril.

Try a past question

A patient presents to emergency department with a BP 220/120 along with headache, seizure , blurred vision and altered sensorium. which of the following drug is used in the management of this patient ?

  1. A. Iv labetalol
  2. B. Iv nicardipine
  3. C. Iv Nitoglycerin
  4. D. Oral methyldopa

Answer: A. Hypertensive encephalopathy needs controlled IV lowering; IV labetalol is a standard first choice.

Heart failure

Must-do31 past questions

Drugs that reduce death in heart failure with reduced EF: ACE inhibitor, ARB or ARNI (sacubitril-valsartan), β-blocker, spironolactone, SGLT2 inhibitor. Digoxin and loop diuretics relieve symptoms only. Stop an ACE inhibitor 36 hours before starting sacubitril-valsartan.

Try a past question

A patient with chronic heart failure is prescribed sacubitril/valsartan. Several days later, he develops facial swelling and difficulty swallowing after mistakenly taking an ACE inhibitor concurrently. Which mechanism best explains this adverse event?

  1. A. Excessive angiotensin II synthesis
  2. B. Combined inhibition of bradykinin degradation
  3. C. Direct mast-cell degranulation by valsartan
  4. D. Increased aldosterone synthesis

Answer: B. Neprilysin and ACE both break down bradykinin; blocking both at once lets bradykinin build up and causes angioedema.

Arrhythmias and Torsades de Pointes

Must-doRepeated26 past questions

Adenosine stops SVT (very short-acting). Amiodarone: pulmonary fibrosis, thyroid problems, corneal deposits. Torsades de pointes: give IV magnesium sulfate. Many drugs prolong QT: class IA and III antiarrhythmics, macrolides, antipsychotics.

Try a past question

Which drug is used for emergency management of Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT) by transiently blockingAV nodal conduction?

  1. A. Verapamil
  2. B. Propranolol
  3. C. Adenosine
  4. D. Amiodarone

Answer: C. Adenosine briefly blocks the AV node, breaking the re-entry circuit that keeps SVT going.

CNS Pharmacology 122 questions

Antiepileptics

Must-doRepeated49 past questions

Drug of choice is asked by seizure type: absence, ethosuximide or valproate; infantile spasms, ACTH, but vigabatrin in tuberous sclerosis; status epilepticus, IV lorazepam first. Valproate is teratogenic (neural tube defects). Phenytoin causes gum hypertrophy and hirsutism.

Try a past question

A 5-month-old infant presents with infantile spasms. Physical examination reveals ash-leaf spots (hypopigmented macules) on the trunk under Wood’s lamp inspection. Which of the following is the first-line drug of choice for this specific child?

  1. A. High-dose ACTH
  2. B. Oral Prednisolone
  3. C. Vigabatrin
  4. D. Sodium Valproate

Answer: C. Ash-leaf spots point to tuberous sclerosis, where vigabatrin is the first choice for infantile spasms.

Parkinsonism

Must-doRepeated8 past questions

Levodopa is given with carbidopa (a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor). COMT inhibitors extend each dose: entacapone works peripherally only, tolcapone peripherally and centrally (liver toxicity). Typical antipsychotics and metoclopramide cause drug-induced parkinsonism.

Try a past question

A 71-year-old man with Parkinson disease develops wearing-off phenomena despite levodopa/carbidopa treatment. An adjunctive drug prolongs levodopa action by inhibiting peripheral and central catechol-O-methyltransferase. Which agent has this dual-site action?

  1. A. Entacapone
  2. B. Tolcapone
  3. C. Selegiline
  4. D. Amantadine

Answer: B. Tolcapone crosses the blood-brain barrier, so it inhibits COMT in both places; entacapone does not.

Antipsychotics

Must-doRepeated29 past questions

Clozapine is for treatment-resistant schizophrenia: agranulocytosis (blood counts), seizures, myocarditis. Typical antipsychotics: dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia, NMS (treat with dantrolene or bromocriptine). Olanzapine: weight gain.

Try a past question

A patient with treatment-resistant schizophrenia is started on clozapine. After several weeks, he develops fever, sore throat, and profound neutropenia. Which mechanism is most closely associated with the major safety concern requiring blood monitoring?

  1. A. Predictable dose-dependent bone-marrow suppression
  2. B. Idiosyncratic severe neutropenia or agranulocytosis
  3. C. Selective inhibition of platelet cyclooxygenase
  4. D. Immune-complex-mediated renal vasculitis as the usual reaction

Answer: B. Clozapine's agranulocytosis is idiosyncratic, not dose-related, which is why blood counts are monitored.

Antidepressants

Must-doRepeated18 past questions

SSRIs are first-line for depression and anxiety. TCA overdose: anticholinergic signs, wide QRS and arrhythmias; treat with sodium bicarbonate. Serotonin syndrome: hyperthermia, clonus, agitation. MAO inhibitors with tyramine-rich food: the cheese reaction.

Try a past question

12 year old boy is brought to ER after accidental ingestion of tricyclic antidepressant medication. He presents with palpitations, dry mouth, mydriasis, and tachycardia. Which of the following is the most appropriate antidote in this scenario?

  1. A. Sodium bicarbonate
  2. B. Physostigmine
  3. C. Flumazenil
  4. D. Naloxone

Answer: A. Sodium bicarbonate reverses the cardiac sodium-channel block of TCAs and narrows the QRS.

General Pharmacology 106 questions

Pharmacokinetics: Vd, half-life, clearance, bioavailability, first-pass

Must-doRepeated46 past questions

Clearance = 0.693 × Vd ÷ t½. Steady state takes 4–5 half-lives, whatever the dose. Loading dose depends on Vd; maintenance dose on clearance. High first-pass drugs (nitroglycerin, lidocaine, propranolol) have low oral bioavailability.

Try a past question

53-year-old patient, weighing 70 kg, has been administered a drug with a half-life of 3 hours and a volume of distribution (Vd) of 100 mL/kg. What is the approximate clearance of this drug?

  1. A. 210 mL/hr
  2. B. 1617 mL/hr
  3. C. 6770 mL/hr
  4. D. 270 mL/hr

Answer: B. Vd = 100 mL/kg × 70 kg = 7,000 mL; clearance = 0.693 × 7,000 ÷ 3 h ≈ 1,617 mL/h.

Dose-response: potency, efficacy, agonists, antagonists, therapeutic index

Must-doRepeated31 past questions

Potency = how much drug you need (position of the curve); efficacy = the maximum effect it can reach (height of the curve). A competitive antagonist shifts the curve right without lowering the maximum; a non-competitive one lowers the maximum. Therapeutic index = TD50 ÷ ED50.

Try a past question

A drug produces a dose-dependent increase in its pharmacological response until a maximum effect is reached. Which pharmacodynamic property is represented by the maximum effect?

  1. A. Potency
  2. B. Efficacy
  3. C. Affinity
  4. D. Bioavailability

Answer: B. The ceiling of the dose-response curve is efficacy; potency only tells you the dose needed.

Enzyme induction/inhibition

Must-doRepeated19 past questions

Inducers: rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbitone, smoking (CYP1A2), chronic alcohol. Inhibitors: macrolides, azoles, ciprofloxacin, cimetidine, valproate, grapefruit, acute alcohol. Exams ask the result: an inhibitor raises the other drug's level (toxicity), an inducer lowers it (failure).

Try a past question

A 34 year old patient, a known case of psychosis was taking clozapine for last 6 months. As per history, he stopped smoking x 4 weeks. He complains of 2 episodes of seizure in last 5 days. What can be the reason for seizure?

  1. A. Not related to smoking cessation
  2. B. It is related to smoking cessation and not clozapine
  3. C. Related to smoking cessation as it decreases CYP1A2
  4. D. Related to smoking cessation as it increases CYP1A2

Answer: C. Smoking induces CYP1A2, which clears clozapine. Stopping smoking removes the induction, clozapine levels rise, and seizures follow.

Endocrine Pharmacology 74 questions

Diabetes drugs

Must-do40 past questions

Metformin: lactic acidosis, B12 deficiency, no hypoglycaemia. SGLT2 inhibitors (-gliflozins): genital fungal infections, euglycaemic ketoacidosis. Glitazones: fluid retention and heart failure. Sulfonylureas and insulin: hypoglycaemia. GLP-1 agonists: weight loss, pancreatitis.

Try a past question

A diabetic patient on therapy presented with itching and whitish vaginal discharge & is experiencing recurrent episodes of discharge. which of the following drug intake lead to this condition ?

  1. A. Metformin
  2. B. Acarbose
  3. C. Rosiglitazone
  4. D. Canagliflozin

Answer: D. SGLT2 inhibitors put glucose in the urine, which feeds recurrent genital candidiasis.

Steroids

Must-doRepeated15 past questions

Long-term steroids: Cushingoid features, osteoporosis, hyperglycaemia, cataract, adrenal suppression. Never stop them suddenly; taper. Dexamethasone has no mineralocorticoid effect; fludrocortisone is mostly mineralocorticoid.

Try a past question

A patient on long-term corticosteroid therapy develops moon face, central obesity, and hypertension. What is the underlying mechanism responsible for these features?

  1. A. Excess glucocorticoid receptor stimulation
  2. B. Increased aldosterone secretion
  3. C. Increased catecholamine release
  4. D. Increased insulin secretion

Answer: A. Moon face and central obesity come from too much glucocorticoid receptor stimulation.

Hematology 38 questions

Anticoagulants: warfarin, heparin, LMWH, DOACs

High-yieldRepeated32 past questions

Heparin is monitored with aPTT and reversed with protamine; warfarin with INR, reversed with vitamin K, plus prothrombin complex or FFP for bleeding. Rivaroxaban and apixaban block factor Xa; dabigatran blocks thrombin. Warfarin skin necrosis happens in protein C deficiency.

Try a past question

A 45-year-old woman is started on warfarin for deep vein thrombosis. On the fourth day, she develops painful erythematous skin lesions with central necrosis. Which underlying condition predisposes her to this complication?

  1. A. Protein C deficiency
  2. B. Factor V Leiden mutation
  3. C. Antithrombin III deficiency
  4. D. Hyperhomocysteinemia

Answer: A. Warfarin lowers protein C before the clotting factors, so the blood is briefly prothrombotic; skin necrosis follows in protein C deficiency.

Respiratory Pharmacology 33 questions

Asthma

High-yieldRepeated26 past questions

Salbutamol (SABA) for quick relief; inhaled steroids are the controller. Montelukast for aspirin-sensitive and exercise asthma. Theophylline has a narrow therapeutic window, and CYP1A2 inhibitors such as ciprofloxacin make it toxic.

Try a past question

A 60-year-old man with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is scheduled for surgery. During induction, he develops severe bronchospasm with wheezing and increased airway resistance. Which inhaled drug is most appropriate for rapid bronchodilation?

  1. A. Salbutamol
  2. B. Ipratropium only
  3. C. Montelukast
  4. D. Budesonide

Answer: A. An inhaled short-acting β2 agonist is the fastest bronchodilator.

COPD

High-yieldRepeated5 past questions

Long-acting bronchodilators are the base: tiotropium (a LAMA) and LABAs. Inhaled steroids are added for frequent exacerbations or high eosinophils. Oxygen therapy and stopping smoking are what improve survival.

GIT Pharmacology 24 questions

Antiemetics

High-yieldRepeated18 past questions

Ondansetron (5-HT3) for chemotherapy vomiting; it prolongs QT. Metoclopramide crosses into the brain and causes dystonia; domperidone does not. Aprepitant (NK1) for delayed chemotherapy vomiting. Motion sickness: hyoscine.

Try a past question

A patient presented with history of few episodes of vomiting and was given an antiemetic drug by the intern. Few hours later, the patient developed abnormal posture. Which of the following is the most likely drug administered?

  1. A. Domperidone
  2. B. Ondansetron
  3. C. Metoclopramide
  4. D. Dexamethasone

Answer: C. Metoclopramide blocks D2 receptors in the basal ganglia and can cause an acute dystonic reaction.
