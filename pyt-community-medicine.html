Epidemiology 223 questions

Measures: incidence, prevalence, mortality rates

Must-doRepeated49 past questions

Incidence = new cases in a period; prevalence = all existing cases (prevalence = incidence × duration). Crude death rate uses the mid-year population (1 July). IMR per 1,000 live births; MMR per 100,000 live births. Case fatality rate shows how deadly a disease is.

Try a past question

  1. A. 2nd August
  2. B. 3rd May
  3. C. 1st July
  4. D. 1st January

Answer: C. Rates use the mid-year population, counted on 1 July.

Study designs: case-control vs cohort vs RCT

Must-doRepeated71 past questions

Case-control: start from disease, look back at exposure; gives the odds ratio; good for rare diseases. Cohort: start from exposure, follow forward; gives incidence and relative risk. RCT: the best evidence; randomisation removes confounding, blinding removes observer bias. Hawthorne effect: people behave differently when watched.

Try a past question

People change their behavior when they know they are being observed. This type of bias is called:

  1. A. Surveillance bias
  2. B. Selection bias
  3. C. Hawthorne effect
  4. D. None of the above

Answer: C. Changing behaviour because one knows one is being observed is the Hawthorne effect.

Risk calculations: relative risk, odds ratio, attributable risk

Must-doRepeated26 past questions

Relative risk = incidence in exposed ÷ incidence in unexposed (cohort). Odds ratio = ad/bc (case-control). Attributable risk = incidence in exposed − incidence in unexposed; as a percentage of the exposed incidence it shows how much could be prevented. NNT = 1 ÷ absolute risk reduction.

Try a past question

In a cohort study, 40 of 400 smokers and 10 of 500 nonsmokers develop a particular disease over 10 years. What is the relative risk of disease among smokers?

  1. A. 2
  2. B. 3
  3. C. 5
  4. D. 10

Answer: C. Smokers 40/400 = 10%, non-smokers 10/500 = 2%: relative risk = 10 ÷ 2 = 5.

Screening: sensitivity, specificity, predictive values

Must-doRepeated33 past questions

Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN), true positive rate, good for ruling out. Specificity = TN/(TN+FP), good for ruling in. PPV = TP/(TP+FP), rises with prevalence; NPV falls as prevalence rises. Sensitivity and specificity do not change with prevalence.

Try a past question

In a population of 10,000, disease prevalence is 1%. A screening test has 80% sensitivity and 90% specificity. What is the Positive Predictive Value (PPV)?

  1. A. 0.074 (7.4%)
  2. B. 0.47 (47%)
  3. C. 0.80 (80%)
  4. D. 0.90 (90%)

Answer: A. Of 10,000 people, 100 are sick: 80 true positives; 9,900 well: 990 false positives. PPV = 80 ÷ 1,070 ≈ 7.5%.

Disease transmission: secondary attack rate, herd immunity

Must-doRepeated20 past questions

Secondary attack rate = new cases among susceptible contacts ÷ total susceptible contacts (the first case excluded), within one incubation period. Herd immunity protects the unvaccinated when enough are immune. Chronic carriers shed the organism for a long time (typhoid: gallbladder).

Try a past question

  1. A. Pseudo carriers
  2. B. Convalescent carriers
  3. C. Healthy carriers
  4. D. Chronic carriers

Answer: D. Chronic carriers keep shedding the organism for a long time, like typhoid carriers.

Natural history of disease

Must-doRepeated22 past questions

Levels of prevention: primordial (stop risk factors from arising, e.g. healthy habits in children), primary (before disease: vaccines, hand washing), secondary (early diagnosis and treatment: screening), tertiary (limit disability and rehabilitate).

  2. B. Secondary prevention
  3. C. Tertiary prevention
  4. D. Primordial prevention

Answer: B. Screening finds disease early, before symptoms: that is secondary prevention.

Communicable Diseases 113 questions

TB

Must-doRepeated20 past questions

Infection (not disease) prevalence is measured with the Mantoux (tuberculin) test. Disease: sputum CBNAAT. Annual risk of TB infection is the best index of TB problem in a community. Treatment under NTEP; notify on Nikshay.

Try a past question

  1. A. Chest X-ray
  2. B. Mantoux test
  3. C. Sputum smear
  4. D. CB-NAAT

Answer: B. The tuberculin (Mantoux) test shows infection, so it is used for infection prevalence surveys.

Rabies prophylaxis

Must-doRepeated19 past questions

Category I (touching, licks on intact skin): no treatment. II (nibbling, minor scratches without bleeding): wound wash and vaccine. III (bleeding bites, licks on broken skin or mucosa, any bat contact): wound wash, vaccine and rabies immunoglobulin. Wash with soap and running water for 15 minutes. IM schedule: days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28 (Essen); ID: 0, 3, 7, 28.

Try a past question

Which of the following does not lead to the transmission of rabies to humans?

  1. A. Ingestion of raw meat of infected animal
  2. B. Direct contact of infected saliva with the mucosa
  3. C. Nibbling by an infected animal on abraded skin
  4. D. Bite of an infected bat

Answer: A. Rabies passes through saliva on mucosa or broken skin; eating meat of an infected animal is not a route.

Malaria

Must-doRepeated23 past questions

Incubation: falciparum about 12 days, vivax 14, malariae 28. Indices: annual parasite incidence (API) per 1,000, slide positivity rate. India: vivax with chloroquine plus 14-day primaquine; falciparum with ACT (artesunate + sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine; artemether-lumefantrine in the north-east).

Measles isolation, outbreaks

Must-doRepeated9 past questions

Infectious from 4 days before to 4 days after the rash appears; isolate for that time. Secondary attack rate about 80%. Vaccine within 72 hours of exposure can protect; immunoglobulin within 6 days. Give vitamin A to every case.

Dengue

Must-doRepeated13 past questions

Aedes aegypti, a day biter that breeds in clean stored water; flight range about 100 m. Larval indices: house index, container index, Breteau index (best). A second infection with a different serotype raises the risk of severe dengue.

HIV basics

Needle-stick risk: hepatitis B about 30%, hepatitis C about 3%, HIV about 0.3%. Post-exposure prophylaxis within 72 hours (best within 2 hours), for 28 days. Mother to child: ART for the mother, nevirapine for the baby.

Biostatistics 77 questions

Mean, median, mode, standard deviation

Must-doRepeated16 past questions

Mean is pulled by outliers; median is best for skewed data; mode is the most frequent value. Measures of spread: range, standard deviation, variance, coefficient of variation (SD/mean × 100). Standard error measures how precise a sample estimate is, not spread.

Normal distribution curve

Must-doRepeated11 past questions

A bell-shaped curve: mean = median = mode. About 68% of values lie within 1 SD, 95% within 2 SD (1.96 exactly), 99.7% within 3 SD. Skewed right: mean > median > mode.

Try a past question

Answer: A. About 68% of values fall within one standard deviation of the mean.

Hypothesis testing (p-value, chi-square)

Must-doRepeated34 past questions

p under 0.05 is called significant. Type I error (α): saying there is a difference when there is none; type II (β): missing a real difference; power = 1 − β. Tests: paired t-test (same group before and after), unpaired t-test (two groups), ANOVA (3+ groups), chi-square (proportions).

Try a past question

  1. A. Paired test
  2. B. Unpaired test
  3. C. ANOVA
  4. D. Chi-square test

Answer: A. The same patients measured before and after the drug: a paired t-test.

Sampling methods

Must-doRepeated9 past questions

Simple random: every unit has an equal chance. Systematic: every kth unit. Stratified: random samples from each subgroup (good when groups differ). Cluster: whole groups such as villages (used in immunisation surveys, 30 × 7). Snowball: subjects refer others (hidden populations).

Try a past question

  1. A. Systematic random sampling
  2. B. Convenience sampling
  3. C. Stratified random sampling
  4. D. Snowball sampling

Answer: D. Recruiting through referrals from earlier subjects is snowball sampling.

Vaccines, Immunization & Cold Chain 70 questions

National Immunization Schedule

Must-doRepeated27 past questions

Pregnancy: Td 2 doses 4 weeks apart, or one booster if fully vaccinated in the last 3 years. Children: BCG, OPV-0 and hepatitis B at birth; pentavalent, OPV and rotavirus at 6, 10, 14 weeks; MR at 9-12 and 16-24 months; Td at 10 and 16 years. Mission Indradhanush covers the routine schedule's diseases.

Vaccine types (live vs killed)

Must-doRepeated25 past questions

Live: BCG, OPV, measles, MR/MMR, rotavirus, yellow fever, varicella, typhoid Ty21a. Killed or subunit: IPV, hepatitis B, DPT components, rabies, typhoid Vi. Live vaccines are avoided in pregnancy and in the immunocompromised. Thiomersal preserves multi-dose DPT, pentavalent and hepatitis B vials.

  3. C. OPV
  4. D. MR

Answer: B. Thiomersal is the preservative in DPT; live vaccines like BCG, OPV and MR do not use it.

Cold chain

Must-doRepeated13 past questions

Most sensitive to heat: OPV (then measles). Most sensitive to freezing: hepatitis B, DPT, Td, pentavalent (check with the shake test). Ice-lined refrigerator +2 to +8 °C; deep freezer −15 to −25 °C. Vaccine vial monitor: discard when the square matches or is darker than the circle.

Screening of Diseases 29 questions

Criteria (Wilson & Jungner)

High-yield4 past questions

Wilson and Jungner: an important health problem, a recognisable early stage, an acceptable test, an effective treatment, and a cost that is worth it. Screening is secondary prevention.

Lead time bias, length bias

Must-do4 past questions

Lead time bias: screening finds disease earlier, so survival looks longer even if death comes at the same time. Length bias: screening picks up slow-growing disease more often. Selection bias: healthier people volunteer for screening.

Health indicators (HDI, IMR, DALY)

High-yield35 past questions

HDI: life expectancy, education and income. DALY = years of life lost + years lived with disability. IMR is the best indicator of a community's health and development. Physical Quality of Life Index: IMR, life expectancy at age 1, literacy.

MCH (Maternal & Child Health) 88 questions

Antenatal care

High-yield14 past questions

At least 4 antenatal visits; PMSMA check on the 9th of each month. Iron-folic acid daily from the second trimester for 180 days; calcium; deworming after the first trimester. Average weight gain in pregnancy about 10-12 kg.

Indicators (IMR, MMR)

High-yield30 past questions

MMR per 100,000 live births; maternal death up to 42 days after the end of pregnancy. Commonest direct cause: haemorrhage; commonest indirect cause in India: anaemia. IMR per 1,000 live births; perinatal mortality from 28 weeks of pregnancy to 7 days after birth.

Nutrition 85 questions

Vitamin deficiencies

Protein-energy malnutrition

High-yield18 past questions

Wasting = low weight-for-height (acute). Stunting = low height-for-age (chronic). Underweight = low weight-for-age (both). SAM: weight-for-height under −3 SD, MUAC under 11.5 cm, or bilateral oedema.

Try a past question

  1. A. Acute malnutrition

  3. C. Chronic malnutrition
  4. D. Concomitant acute and chronic malnutrition

Answer: A. Low weight for height is wasting, a sign of acute malnutrition.

Occupational Health 17 questions

Occupational health

Asked17 past questions

Pneumoconioses: silicosis (the commonest in India; mining, sandblasting), asbestosis (mesothelioma), byssinosis (cotton, Monday fever), bagassosis (sugarcane). Lead poisoning: basophilic stippling, Burton line, wrist drop. ESI Act covers workers' medical and cash benefits. Needle-stick HIV risk is the lowest of the blood-borne viruses.
