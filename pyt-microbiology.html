Bacteriology 228 questions

Staphylococcus and Streptococcus

Must-doRepeated59 past questions

Staph aureus: coagulase positive, golden colonies, catalase positive. Lancefield grouping uses the C carbohydrate of the cell wall. Group A strep: bacitracin sensitive, rheumatic fever and glomerulonephritis. Pneumococcus: optochin sensitive, bile soluble, lancet-shaped diplococci. Enterococcus grows in 6.5% salt and bile.

Try a past question

Beta-hemolytic streptococci isolated from a throat swab are further classified into Lancefield groups. This classification is based on which component of the bacterial cell wall?

  1. A. M protein
  2. B. C carbohydrate
  3. C. Lipoteichoic acid
  4. D. Hemolysin type

Answer: B. Lancefield groups are based on the C carbohydrate antigen in the streptococcal cell wall.

E. coli, Salmonella, Shigella

Must-doRepeated40 past questions

Typhoid: blood culture in week 1 (the best test), Widal from week 2, stool and urine cultures in weeks 3-4; the gallbladder holds carriers. Shigella: non-motile, the lowest infective dose. E. coli types: ETEC (traveller's diarrhoea), EHEC O157:H7 (HUS). Salmonella makes H2S.

Try a past question

A twelve-year-old boy presents with high-grade fever, abdominal pain, coated tongue, and splenomegaly for 5 days. What is the best diagnostic method in the first week of suspected enteric fever?

  1. A. Stool culture
  2. B. Urine culture
  3. C. Widal test
  4. D. Blood culture

Answer: D. Blood culture is most often positive in the first week of enteric fever.

Vibrio cholerae

Must-doRepeated22 past questions

Comma-shaped, darting motility, TCBS agar gives yellow colonies (sucrose fermenter). Alkaline peptone water for enrichment. Cholera toxin raises cAMP: rice-water stools. O1 (El Tor) and O139 cause epidemics. Treat with ORS; doxycycline or azithromycin shortens it.

Try a past question

Growth on thiosulfate citrate bile salts sucrose (TCBS) agar shows yellow colonies. Identify the organism.

  1. A. Vibrio cholerae
  2. B. Helicobacter pylori
  3. C. Campylobacter jejuni
  4. D. Aeromonas

Answer: A. Vibrio cholerae ferments sucrose and makes yellow colonies on TCBS agar.

Mycobacterium tuberculosis

Must-doRepeated34 past questions

CBNAAT (Xpert MTB/RIF) detects TB and rifampicin resistance in about 2 hours. Line probe assay: rifampicin and isoniazid resistance in a day or two. LJ culture takes 6-8 weeks; liquid culture (MGIT) is faster. Cord factor makes serpentine cords; niacin test positive.

  1. A. Line Probe Assay
  2. B. Lowenstein-Jensen culture
  3. C. Xpert MTB/RIF
  4. D. iGRA

Answer: C. Xpert MTB/RIF (CBNAAT) gives TB and rifampicin resistance in about 2 hours.

Clostridium and its toxins

Must-doRepeated22 past questions

C. botulinum: blocks acetylcholine release, descending flaccid paralysis (canned food, honey in infants). C. tetani: blocks inhibitory transmitters (GABA, glycine), spastic paralysis, drumstick spores. C. perfringens: gas gangrene, Nagler reaction, double zone of haemolysis. C. difficile: pseudomembranous colitis after antibiotics.

Try a past question

A toxin causes flaccid paralysis by blocking the release of acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction. Identify the organism.

  1. A. Clostridium botulinum
  2. B. Clostridium tetani
  3. C. Corynebacterium diphtheriae
  4. D. Vibrio cholerae

Answer: A. Botulinum toxin stops acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction, causing flaccid paralysis.

Pseudomonas

Must-doRepeated5 past questions

Pseudomonas aeruginosa: oxidase positive, non-fermenter, blue-green pigment (pyocyanin), grape-like smell. Infects burns, cystic fibrosis lungs, ventilators, swimmer's ear. Treat with anti-pseudomonal drugs (piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, aminoglycosides).

General Microbiology 105 questions

Gram and Ziehl-Neelsen staining

Must-do27 past questions

Gram stain: crystal violet, iodine, decolouriser, safranin. Ziehl-Neelsen (acid-fast): TB with 20% sulfuric acid, leprosy with 5%. Albert stain: metachromatic granules of diphtheria. India ink: Cryptococcus capsule. Giemsa: malaria and blood parasites. Gomori methenamine silver: Pneumocystis and fungi.

Try a past question

The special stain used to identify metachromatic granules of Corynebacterium diphtheriae in a throat swab is:

  1. A. Albert’s stain
  2. B. Gram’s stain
  3. C. Giemsa stain
  4. D. India ink stain

Answer: A. Albert's stain shows the metachromatic (volutin) granules of Corynebacterium diphtheriae.

Culture media

Must-do40 past questions

Selective media: Thayer-Martin (Neisseria), TCBS (Vibrio, yellow colonies), Lowenstein-Jensen (TB), Löffler serum slope and potassium tellurite (diphtheria), MacConkey (lactose fermenters pink), XLD/DCA (Salmonella, Shigella), Bordet-Gengou (pertussis), Sabouraud (fungi). Chocolate agar: Haemophilus (needs factors X and V).

Try a past question

A clinical history of meningococcal septicemia is present. Which selective medium is preferred for isolating neisseria from specimens with contaminating flora?

  1. A. Deoxycholate citrate agar
  2. B. Blood agar
  3. C. Lowenstein-Jensen medium
  4. D. Modified Thayer-Martin medium

Answer: D. Modified Thayer-Martin medium has antibiotics that suppress normal flora, so Neisseria can be isolated.

Sterilization methods

Must-do21 past questions

Autoclave: 121 °C for 15 minutes at 15 psi (spore test: Geobacillus stearothermophilus). Hot air oven: 160 °C for 2 hours (glassware, powders, oils). Tyndallisation: steaming on 3 days for sugar and gelatin media. Ethylene oxide and plasma (hydrogen peroxide) for heat-sensitive equipment. Prions need special methods.

Try a past question

A sterilization method is required for heat-labile media containing sugars and gelatin. Which method is preferred?

  1. A. Autoclaving
  2. B. Hot air oven
  3. C. Tyndallization
  4. D. Incineration

Answer: C. Tyndallisation (intermittent steaming) sterilises media with sugars and gelatin that autoclaving would damage.

Disinfectants

Must-do10 past questions

Blood spills: sodium hypochlorite (1% for spills). Glutaraldehyde 2%: endoscopes (high-level disinfection). Alcohol 70% works better than absolute alcohol. Chlorhexidine for skin. Spores are killed only by sterilants (glutaraldehyde with long contact, ethylene oxide, autoclaving).

Try a past question

What is the disinfectant used for blood spillage in hospital settings?

  1. A. Lysol
  2. B. Ethyl alcohol
  3. C. Isopropyl alcohol
  4. D. Sodium hypochlorite

Answer: D. Sodium hypochlorite inactivates blood-borne viruses and is the standard for blood spills.

Bacterial genetics

Must-do4 past questions

Transformation: naked DNA taken up. Transduction: DNA carried by a bacteriophage. Conjugation: DNA passed through a sex pilus (plasmids, drug resistance). Lysogenic conversion gives toxins: diphtheria, botulinum, cholera, Shiga-like, scarlet fever toxins.

Try a past question

A bacteriophage attaches to a bacterial cell and transfers bacterial DNA into it. Which of the following processes of genetic transfer is being demonstrated?

  1. A. Transformation
  2. B. Conjugation
  3. C. Transduction
  4. D. Transposition

Answer: C. DNA moved from one bacterium to another by a bacteriophage is transduction.

Virology 144 questions

Hepatitis viruses and markers

High-yieldRepeated23 past questions

HBsAg: current infection. IgM anti-HBc: acute infection, and the only marker in the window period (HBsAg gone, anti-HBs not yet up). HBeAg: high infectivity. Anti-HBs alone: vaccinated. Hepatitis A and E spread faeco-orally; E is dangerous in pregnancy. Hepatitis D needs hepatitis B.

Try a past question

The window period in hepatitis B infection refers to which serological profile?

  1. A. HBsAg positive, Anti-HBs positive
  2. B. HBsAg negative, Anti-HBs negative, Anti-HBc IgM positive
  3. C. HBsAg positive, Anti-HBc IgG positive
  4. D. Anti-HBs positive only

Answer: B. In the window period HBsAg and anti-HBs are both negative and only IgM anti-HBc is positive.

HIV structure and diagnosis

High-yieldRepeated29 past questions

HIV-1 subtype C is the commonest in India. gp120 binds CD4, with CCR5 or CXCR4 co-receptors. India's testing uses three different rapid tests (ELISA-based). Infants: DNA PCR, since maternal antibodies last up to 18 months. Viral load monitors treatment.

Try a past question

What is the most common HIV-1 subtype seen in India?

  1. A. A
  2. B. B
  3. C. C
  4. D. D

Answer: C. Subtype C causes most HIV-1 infections in India.

Herpes viruses

High-yieldRepeated47 past questions

EBV: infectious mononucleosis (Paul-Bunnell heterophile test, atypical lymphocytes), Burkitt lymphoma, nasopharyngeal cancer. CMV: owl's eye inclusions, the commonest congenital infection. HSV: Tzanck smear (multinucleated giant cells), HSV-1 encephalitis in the temporal lobe. VZV: chickenpox and shingles. HHV-8: Kaposi sarcoma.

  4. D. Paul-Bunnell test

Answer: D. The Paul-Bunnell test detects heterophile antibodies in infectious mononucleosis.

Measles, Rubella (TORCH)

High-yieldRepeated17 past questions

Congenital rubella: PDA, cataract, deafness. CMV: periventricular calcification, deafness. Toxoplasma: hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis, scattered brain calcification. Syphilis: Hutchinson teeth, saddle nose, snuffles. HSV: skin, eye and mouth lesions, encephalitis.

Parasitology 121 questions

Malaria

High-yieldRepeated19 past questions

Falciparum: multiple delicate rings, appliqué (accolé) forms, banana-shaped gametocytes, Maurer dots. Vivax and ovale: Schüffner dots, enlarged red cells, hypnozoites (relapses; primaquine). Malariae: band forms, 72-hour fever. Thick smear to find, thin smear to identify species.

Try a past question

A peripheral smear shows multiple delicate ring forms within a single RBC and appliqué (accolé) forms. Which species is most likely?

  1. A. Plasmodium vivax
  2. B. Plasmodium malariae
  3. C. Plasmodium ovale
  4. D. Plasmodium falciparum

Answer: D. Several fine rings in one red cell and appliqué forms are typical of P. falciparum.

Filariasis

High-yieldRepeated14 past questions

Wuchereria bancrofti, spread by Culex; microfilariae show nocturnal periodicity (take blood at night). The membrane filtration concentration method is the most sensitive for microfilariae. Immunochromatographic card test detects antigen by day. DEC with albendazole for mass treatment.

Try a past question

Which among the following is the most sensitive test for detecting microfilariae in blood?

  1. A. DEC provocation test
  2. B. Immunofluorescence test
  3. C. Thick blood film
  4. D. Membrane filter concentration technique

Answer: D. Filtering a large blood volume through a membrane concentrates microfilariae, making it the most sensitive method.

Amoebiasis

High-yieldRepeated10 past questions

Entamoeba histolytica: cysts with 4 nuclei, trophozoites with ingested red cells. Flask-shaped colonic ulcers; amoebic liver abscess (anchovy sauce pus, right lobe). Treat with metronidazole, then a luminal agent (diloxanide, paromomycin).

  3. C. Trichomonas
  4. D. Plasmodium

Answer: A. The mature cyst of Entamoeba histolytica has four nuclei.

Toxoplasmosis

High-yieldRepeated11 past questions

Cats are the definitive host; humans get it from cat faeces (oocysts) or undercooked meat (tissue cysts). Tachyzoites cross the placenta. Congenital triad: hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis, intracranial calcification. In HIV: ring-enhancing brain lesions.

Try a past question

A pregnant woman acquires primary toxoplasmosis. What is the infective stage transmitted to the fetus?

  1. A. Tachyzoite
  2. B. Bradyzoite
  3. C. Oocyst
  4. D. Sporozoite

Answer: A. Tachyzoites, the rapidly dividing form, cross the placenta to the fetus.

Cestodes, Nematodes and Trematodes

High-yieldRepeated61 past questions

Hookworm enters through the skin of bare feet; causes iron deficiency anaemia. Hydatid cyst (Echinococcus granulosus, dogs): rupture can cause anaphylaxis. Taenia solium: neurocysticercosis (pork). Diphyllobothrium latum: B12 deficiency. Strongyloides: autoinfection, hyperinfection with steroids. Enterobius: perianal itch, Scotch tape test.

Immunology and Vaccines 95 questions

Vaccine types

High-yieldRepeated13 past questions

Live attenuated: BCG, OPV, measles, MMR, rotavirus, varicella, yellow fever, oral typhoid. Killed/inactivated: IPV, rabies, hepatitis A. Subunit/recombinant: hepatitis B, HPV. Toxoids: tetanus, diphtheria. Conjugate: Hib, pneumococcal, meningococcal.

Which vaccine is a live attenuated vaccine?

  1. A. Hepatitis B vaccine
  2. B. Rabies vaccine
  3. C. Inactivated polio vaccine
  4. D. MMR vaccine

Answer: D. MMR is live attenuated; hepatitis B, rabies and IPV are not.

Cold chain

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

Routine vaccines are stored at +2 to +8 °C. OPV is the most heat-sensitive and can be frozen (−15 to −25 °C). Freeze-sensitive: hepatitis B, DPT, Td, pentavalent, IPV, PCV, rotavirus (never freeze; shake test if in doubt).

  2. B. 0 to 2 degrees C
  3. C. 2 to 8 degrees C
  4. D. 8 to 15 degrees C

Answer: C. Most routine vaccines are kept at +2 to +8 °C.

Hypersensitivity

High-yieldRepeated14 past questions

Type I: IgE, mast cells (anaphylaxis, hydatid cyst rupture, asthma). Type II: antibodies against cells (transfusion reactions, Rh disease). Type III: immune complexes (serum sickness, Arthus, post-strep GN). Type IV: T cells, delayed (Mantoux, contact dermatitis, granulomas).

Immunodeficiency

High-yieldRepeated19 past questions

B cell: X-linked agammaglobulinaemia (Bruton, BTK), recurrent bacterial infections after 6 months. T cell: DiGeorge (22q11, no thymus, low calcium). Both: SCID (adenosine deaminase deficiency). Phagocyte: chronic granulomatous disease (NADPH oxidase, NBT test). Wiskott-Aldrich: eczema, low platelets, infections.

  2. B. DiGeorge syndrome
  3. C. PNP deficiency
  4. D. Chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis

Answer: A. X-linked agammaglobulinaemia is a B-cell defect; DiGeorge is a T-cell defect.

Antigen-antibody reactions

High-yieldRepeated21 past questions

Epitope: the part of the antigen that binds; paratope: the part of the antibody that binds it. IgM appears first in a primary response and is the biggest (pentamer); IgG crosses the placenta; IgA is in secretions; IgE in allergy and worms. Precipitation, agglutination and complement fixation are classic reactions.

Try a past question

The part of the antigen that reacts with the immune system is called as?

  1. A. Epitope
  2. B. Idiotope
  3. C. Clone
  4. D. Paratope

Answer: A. The epitope is the small part of an antigen that the antibody or receptor recognises.

ELISA, PCR

High-yieldRepeated20 past questions

ELISA: sensitive screening (HIV, hepatitis). Western blot: confirmation. PCR finds DNA directly and is the test of choice for many viruses, including monkeypox (mpox), from lesion swabs. RT-PCR for RNA viruses. Real-time PCR also measures viral load.

Try a past question

Diagnostic test for monkeypox virus is ?

  1. A. ELISA
  2. B. PCR
  3. C. Hemagglutination test
  4. D. Serum Ab test

Answer: B. Mpox is confirmed by PCR on a lesion sample.

Candida

High-yield14 past questions

Candida albicans: germ tube test positive within 2 hours in serum, pseudohyphae, chlamydospores on cornmeal agar. Risk: diabetes, antibiotics, steroids, HIV. Candida auris: drug-resistant, spreads in hospitals.

Dimorphic fungi

High-yield26 past questions

Dimorphic fungi: mould in the cold, yeast in the body. Histoplasma (bird and bat droppings, inside macrophages), Blastomyces (broad-based budding), Coccidioides (spherules), Paracoccidioides (ship's wheel), Sporothrix (rose gardener's disease, cigar-shaped yeast, spreads along lymphatics), Talaromyces marneffei.

Aspergillus vs Mucor

High-yield32 past questions

Aspergillus: septate hyphae branching at acute angles (45°); fungus ball, ABPA in asthma, invasive disease in neutropenia; voriconazole. Mucor/Rhizopus: broad aseptate hyphae at right angles; rhino-orbito-cerebral disease in diabetic ketoacidosis; amphotericin B and surgery.

  3. C. Candidiasis
  4. D. Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis

Answer: D. Septate acute-angle hyphae in an asthmatic with worsening symptoms point to allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis.

Applied Microbiology 25 questions

Nosocomial infections

AskedNEET-PG · INI-CET10 past questions

Hospital-acquired: appears 48 hours or more after admission. Commonest: urinary infections (catheters), then surgical site infections, pneumonia (ventilators) and line infections. Hand hygiene is the single most important prevention. MRSA, ESBL organisms, Acinetobacter and Candida auris are the problem bugs.

Biomedical waste

AskedNEET-PG · INI-CET5 past questions

Yellow: infectious and anatomical waste (incinerate). Red: contaminated recyclable plastics (autoclave). White puncture-proof: sharps. Blue: glass and metal implants. Do not store untreated waste over 48 hours.

Infection control

AskedNEET-PG · INI-CET12 past questions

Biosafety levels: BSL-1 harmless organisms; BSL-2 most routine pathogens; BSL-3 airborne serious ones like M. tuberculosis; BSL-4 the deadliest viruses (Ebola). Standard precautions for everyone; contact, droplet and airborne precautions by organism (N95 for TB).

Try a past question

Which biosafety level laboratory is required for handling Mycobacterium tuberculosis?

  1. A. BSL-1
  2. B. BSL-2
  3. C. BSL-3
  4. D. BSL-4

Answer: C. Culturing M. tuberculosis, an airborne pathogen, needs a BSL-3 laboratory.
