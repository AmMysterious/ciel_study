Gastrointestinal Surgery 271 questions

Esophagus: achalasia, carcinoma

Must-doRepeated73 past questions

From the upper incisors: the cricopharynx is about 15 cm, the aortic arch 25 cm, the GE junction 40 cm. Achalasia: loss of the nerve plexus (low NO), bird-beak on barium; Heller myotomy or POEM. Oesophageal cancer: squamous in the middle third, adenocarcinoma (Barrett) at the lower end.

  2. B. 25 cm
  3. C. 40 cm
  4. D. 45 cm

Answer: C. The gastro-oesophageal junction is about 40 cm from the upper incisors.

Stomach: peptic ulcer, carcinoma

Must-doRepeated46 past questions

Bleeding ulcer: a posterior duodenal ulcer erodes the gastroduodenal artery; a lesser-curve gastric ulcer, the left gastric artery. Perforation: air under the diaphragm; Graham patch repair. Gastric cancer: signet ring cells, Virchow node, Krukenberg tumour; H. pylori is a risk factor.

  1. A. Gastroduodenal
  2. B. Inferior pancreaticoduodenal
  3. C. Left gastric
  4. D. Right gastroepiploic

Answer: C. The left gastric artery runs along the upper lesser curvature, so a high lesser-curve ulcer bleeds from it.

Small bowel obstruction

Must-doRepeated26 past questions

Commonest cause: adhesions after surgery (hernia where no previous surgery). Colicky pain, vomiting, distension, no flatus; X-ray shows dilated loops and air-fluid levels. Start with drip and suck; operate if strangulation is suspected. Gallstone ileus: pneumobilia, small bowel obstruction and an ectopic gallstone (Rigler triad).

Try a past question

  1. A. Intussusception
  2. B. Gallstone ileus
  3. C. Adhesive intestinal obstruction
  4. D. Sigmoid volvulus

Answer: B. Air in the bile tree, small bowel obstruction and a stone in the bowel make up Rigler's triad of gallstone ileus.

Appendix

Must-doRepeated28 past questions

Pain starts around the umbilicus and moves to the right iliac fossa (McBurney point). Alvarado score (MANTRELS): migration 1, anorexia 1, nausea or vomiting 1, tenderness in RIF 2, rebound 1, raised temperature 1, leucocytosis 2, shift to the left 1. Score 7 or more: likely appendicitis.

Try a past question

  1. A. Migratory pain - score 1
  2. B. Leukocytosis - score 2
  3. C. Nausea and vomiting - score 1
  4. D. Rebound tenderness - score 2

Answer: D. In the Alvarado score rebound tenderness scores 1, not 2; tenderness in the right iliac fossa and leucocytosis score 2.

Colorectal carcinoma

Must-doRepeated26 past questions

Right-sided cancers present with anaemia; left-sided with altered bowel habit and obstruction. CEA for follow-up. Below the dentate line the anal canal drains to the superficial inguinal nodes; above it, to the internal iliac and pararectal nodes. Familial polyposis: APC gene.

  2. B. Deep inguinal lymph nodes
  3. C. Internal iliac lymph nodes
  4. D. External iliac lymph nodes

Answer: A. The anal verge lies below the dentate line, which drains to the superficial inguinal nodes.

Hernias

Must-doRepeated59 past questions

Indirect inguinal hernia: lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels, through the deep ring, commonest in all ages. Direct: medial, through Hesselbach triangle. Femoral: below and lateral to the pubic tubercle, high risk of strangulation, commoner in women. Lichtenstein tension-free mesh repair (permanent polypropylene, large-pore, lightweight).

Try a past question

  1. A. Lightweight
  2. B. Big Pore
  3. C. Absorbable
  4. D. Artificial

Answer: C. An absorbable mesh dissolves and the hernia comes back; repair meshes are permanent.

Surgical Basics 211 questions

Wound healing

Must-doRepeated33 past questions

Healing by first intention (clean, sutured) or second intention (left open, heals by granulation). Phases: inflammation, proliferation, remodelling; collagen type III is laid down first, then replaced by type I. A pink serosanguinous discharge from a laparotomy wound around day 5-8 is the warning sign of burst abdomen (dehiscence).

Try a past question

  1. A. copious serosanguinous discharge
  2. B. severe abdominal pain
  3. C. hypotension
  4. D. rebound tenderness.

Answer: A. A sudden copious serosanguinous discharge is the classic sign that the deep layers have given way.

Shock and fluid resuscitation

Must-doRepeated38 past questions

ATLS classes of blood loss: I under 15%, II 15-30% (pulse up), III 30-40% (BP falls, confusion), IV over 40%. Class III and IV need blood. Start with 1 L warm crystalloid; avoid big volumes. Hypovolaemic shock is the commonest in trauma.

Burns

Must-doRepeated33 past questions

Rule of nines for burn area (palm of the patient's hand = 1%). Parkland: 4 mL × kg × % burn in 24 hours, half in the first 8 hours from the time of burn. Current ATLS starts adults at 2 mL × kg × % (Ringer lactate) and adjusts to urine output of 0.5 mL/kg/hour. Electrical burns need more.

Try a past question

  1. A. 4000 mL
  2. B. 3000 mL
  3. C. 2000 mL
  4. D. 5000 mL

Answer: C. ATLS: 2 mL × 50 kg × 20% = 2,000 mL in the first 24 hours (Parkland's 4 mL would give 4,000).

Surgical infections: SSI, necrotizing fasciitis, sepsis

Must-doRepeated51 past questions

Wound classes: clean, clean-contaminated, contaminated, dirty. Necrotising fasciitis: pain out of proportion, crepitus, rapid spread; urgent wide debridement plus antibiotics. Give prophylactic antibiotics within 60 minutes before the incision.

Suture materials & instruments

Must-doRepeated42 past questions

Absorbable: catgut, polyglactin (Vicryl), polydioxanone (PDS, slowest). Non-absorbable: silk, nylon, polypropylene (Prolene, used for vessels and hernia mesh). Blades: No. 11 pointed for stabbing and incision and drainage; No. 10 and 22 for skin incisions; No. 15 for small precise cuts.

Answer: B. The pointed No. 11 blade is used for stab incisions such as draining an abscess.

Trauma & Critical Care 126 questions

Primary survey (ABCDE)

Must-do42 past questions

Primary survey, in order: A airway with cervical spine protection, B breathing, C circulation and bleeding control, D disability (GCS, pupils), E exposure (and prevent hypothermia). Fix each problem as it is found. Then the secondary survey: head-to-toe and history (AMPLE).

Trimodal mortality

Must-do3 past questions

Trimodal death in trauma: first peak within minutes (brain, heart, great vessel injury; only prevention helps), second peak in the first hours (the golden hour, where ATLS saves lives), third peak days to weeks later (sepsis, multi-organ failure).

Abdominal trauma (FAST, DPL)

Must-do41 past questions

FAST scan for free fluid in an unstable patient. Unstable with a positive FAST, or not responding to fluids: laparotomy. Stable: CT abdomen. The spleen is the most injured organ in blunt trauma; the small bowel in penetrating trauma.

  4. D. Abdominal compression

Answer: B. Shock after blunt trauma that does not respond to fluids means ongoing bleeding: go straight to laparotomy.

Compartment syndrome

Must-do5 past questions

Pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch are the early signs; pulses are lost late. Compartment pressure over 30 mmHg, or within 30 mmHg of the diastolic BP. Treat with urgent fasciotomy. Common after tibial fractures and tight casts.

Blood transfusion protocols

Must-do13 past questions

Cryoprecipitate: fibrinogen, factor VIII, von Willebrand factor, factor XIII. FFP: all clotting factors. Massive transfusion: give plasma, platelets and red cells about 1:1:1. Platelets are stored at 20-24 °C with agitation, red cells at 2-6 °C.

  3. C. Thromboplastin
  4. D. Factor X

Answer: A. Cryoprecipitate is rich in factor VIII (and fibrinogen).

Hepatobiliary & Pancreas 123 questions

Gallstones & complications

Must-do38 past questions

Ultrasound is the investigation of choice for gallstones. Cholecystitis: Murphy sign, treat with early laparoscopic cholecystectomy. HIDA scan when ultrasound is unclear. Charcot triad of cholangitis: fever, jaundice, right upper pain (add shock and confusion for Reynolds pentad).

  1. A. Percutaneous transhepatic cholangiography (PTC)
  2. B. Oral cholecystography (OCG)
  3. C. Ultrasound
  4. D. Intravenous cholangiogram

Answer: C. Ultrasound is the first-line investigation for the gallbladder: cheap, safe and accurate for stones.

Obstructive jaundice

Must-do29 past questions

Obstructive jaundice: conjugated bilirubin, dark urine, pale stools, itching, high ALP. Painless progressive jaundice with a palpable gallbladder (Courvoisier law) suggests cancer of the pancreatic head, not stones. MRCP to map the ducts; ERCP to drain or stent.

  1. A. Unconjugated bilirubin
  2. B. Conjugated bilirubin
  3. C. No bilirubin elevation
  4. D. Only delta bilirubin

Answer: B. A blocked bile duct backs up bilirubin that the liver has already conjugated.

Pancreatitis

Must-do38 past questions

Commonest causes: gallstones, then alcohol. Diagnosis needs 2 of 3: typical pain, amylase or lipase over 3 times normal, imaging. Lipase is more specific. Severity: Ranson, BISAP, the revised Atlanta classification. Treat with fluids, pain relief and early feeding; ERCP if there is cholangitis.

  1. A. Idiopathic
  2. B. Alcohol
  3. C. Biliary tract disease
  4. D. Pancreatic infection

Answer: C. Gallstones are the commonest cause of acute pancreatitis, followed by alcohol.

Choledochal cyst, CBD stones

Must-do13 past questions

Choledochal cysts: Todani classification, type I (fusiform dilatation of the bile duct) is the commonest. Triad: pain, jaundice, a mass. Excised with a hepaticojejunostomy because of cancer risk. CBD stones: ERCP to clear them, then cholecystectomy.

Endocrine Surgery 88 questions

Thyroid: nodules, carcinoma, Graves

Must-doINI-CET59 past questions

A thyroid nodule is investigated first with TSH, ultrasound and fine-needle aspiration (Bethesda system). Papillary cancer is the commonest (Orphan Annie nuclei, psammoma bodies, spreads to nodes). Medullary: calcitonin, MEN2. Graves eye signs: Dalrymple, von Graefe, Stellwag, Möbius. Pemberton sign is for retrosternal goitre.

Try a past question

  1. A. Stellwag sign
  2. B. Dalrymple sign
  3. C. Pemberton's sign
  4. D. Mobius's sign

Answer: C. Pemberton sign (facial congestion on raising the arms) shows a retrosternal goitre, not Graves eye disease.

Hyperparathyroidism

Must-doINI-CET16 past questions

Primary hyperparathyroidism: high calcium and high PTH; usually a single adenoma. 'Bones, stones, groans and moans'. Localise the adenoma with a Tc-99m sestamibi scan before surgery. After parathyroid surgery watch for low calcium (hungry bone syndrome).

  1. A. TC99 sestamibi scan
  2. B. Early arthritis
  3. C. MIBG
  4. D. TC99 DTPA

Answer: A. A sestamibi scan finds the overactive parathyroid adenoma before surgery.

MEN syndromes

Must-doRepeatedINI-CET9 past questions

MEN1: parathyroid, pituitary, pancreatic tumours. MEN2A: medullary thyroid cancer, phaeochromocytoma, hyperparathyroidism. MEN2B: medullary thyroid cancer, phaeochromocytoma, mucosal neuromas. In MEN2, remove the phaeochromocytoma before the thyroid.

Breast Surgery 82 questions

Breast carcinoma

Must-doRepeated62 past questions

Commonest site: the upper outer quadrant. Triple assessment: examination, imaging (mammogram over 35, ultrasound under 35), core biopsy. Risk: BRCA1 and 2, Li-Fraumeni (p53), early menarche, late menopause, no children. Treatment depends on stage and receptors (ER/PR, HER2: trastuzumab).

Try a past question

Which of the following is associated with the least risk of developing breast cancer?

  1. A. BRCA1
  2. B. Li-Fraumeni syndrome
  3. C. Ataxia telangiectasia
  4. D. BRCA2

Answer: C. BRCA1, BRCA2 and Li-Fraumeni carry high lifetime risks; ataxia-telangiectasia carriers have only a moderate rise.

Fibroadenoma vs carcinoma

Must-doRepeated10 past questions

Fibroadenoma: a breast mouse, a mobile painless smooth lump in young women (15-25). Confirm with ultrasound and core biopsy; can be left if small. Phyllodes tumour: older, grows fast, leaf-like pattern; needs wide excision.

  1. A. Prepubertal
  2. B. Old females
  3. C. Young females
  4. D. Neonates

Answer: C. Fibroadenoma is commonest in young women, in their late teens and twenties.

Sentinel node biopsy

Must-doRepeated3 past questions

Sentinel node biopsy stages the axilla in clinically node-negative early breast cancer, using blue dye and/or radioactive tracer. If the sentinel node is negative, axillary clearance is avoided (less lymphoedema).

Urology 154 questions

Renal stones

High-yield22 past questions

Calcium oxalate is the commonest stone. Struvite (staghorn) with urease-making bacteria (Proteus). Uric acid stones are radiolucent; dissolve with urine alkalinisation. Ammonium urate stones with laxative abuse. Non-contrast CT is the investigation of choice. Obstructed infected kidney: drain urgently (stent or nephrostomy).

Try a past question

Which of the following types of renal stones are produced by laxative abuse?

  1. A. Struvite e
  2. B. Ammonium urate
  3. C. Uric acid
  4. D. Calcium oxalate

Answer: B. Laxative abuse causes bicarbonate loss and low urine volume, which favours ammonium urate stones.

Prostate: BPH vs carcinoma

High-yield41 past questions

BPH grows in the transitional zone; cancer in the peripheral zone (felt on rectal examination). PSA for screening and follow-up. BPH: α-blockers (tamsulosin) and 5α-reductase inhibitors (finasteride), TURP for failed drugs. Prostate cancer spreads to bone (osteoblastic) and is graded by Gleason score.

Try a past question

Which of the following compounds form endogenous prostatic calculi?

  1. A. Calcium oxalate
  2. B. Struvite
  3. C. Calcium phosphate
  4. D. Uric acid

Answer: C. Endogenous prostatic stones form in the gland itself and are mostly calcium phosphate.

Testicular tumors

High-yield32 past questions

Seminoma is the commonest testicular tumour; very sensitive to radiotherapy. Markers: AFP (yolk sac), β-hCG (choriocarcinoma), LDH. Undescended testis raises the risk. Orchidectomy through an inguinal incision, never the scrotum. Lymph spread goes first to the para-aortic nodes.

  3. C. obturator lymph node
  4. D. precaval lymph node

Answer: A. The testis develops near the kidney, so its lymph drains first to the para-aortic nodes.

Hydronephrosis

High-yield10 past questions

Bilateral hydronephrosis: an obstruction at or below the bladder (posterior urethral valves, BPH, stricture, phimosis). Unilateral: a cause above the bladder (stone, PUJ obstruction, retrocaval ureter). An obstructed infected kidney needs urgent drainage.

  3. C. Urgent urinary decompression with DJ stenting or percutaneous nephrostomy
  4. D. Elective nephrectomy

Answer: C. Infection behind an obstructing stone is an emergency: drain the kidney first with a stent or nephrostomy.

Vascular Surgery 66 questions

Varicose veins

High-yield17 past questions

Great saphenous vein incompetence is commonest. Tests: Trendelenburg (saphenofemoral junction), Perthes (deep veins), Doppler or duplex is the investigation of choice. Treatment now: endovenous radiofrequency or laser ablation; sclerotherapy for small veins. Complication: venous ulcer above the medial malleolus.

Answer: B. Endovenous radiofrequency ablation has replaced stripping and ligation as the preferred treatment.

DVT

High-yield15 past questions

Virchow triad: stasis, vessel wall injury, high clotting tendency (surgery, pill, pregnancy, cancer). Duplex ultrasound to diagnose; D-dimer rules out when low. Treat with low molecular weight heparin, then an oral anticoagulant. The fear is a pulmonary embolism.

  1. A. Wound infection
  2. B. Thrombocytosis
  3. C. Pelvic abscess
  4. D. Venous thrombosis

Answer: D. Recent surgery plus the pill (Virchow triad) and a swollen tender leg point to deep vein thrombosis.

Peripheral arterial disease

High-yield24 past questions

Intermittent claudication; rest pain and gangrene mean critical ischaemia. Ankle-brachial index under 0.9 is abnormal (very high in calcified vessels, as in diabetes). Acute ischaemia: the 6 Ps (pain, pallor, pulselessness, paraesthesia, paralysis, perishing cold). Buerger disease: young male smokers.

Neurosurgery 61 questions

Raised ICP

AskedRepeated7 past questions

Cushing triad of raised pressure: high BP, slow pulse, irregular breathing. Treat: head up 30°, keep CO2 normal-low, mannitol or hypertonic saline. Do not give mannitol when serum osmolality is over 320 mOsm/kg.

Try a past question

A patient is brought to casualty with head injury. In which of the following conditions, mannitol should not be used to control increased intra-cranial pressure?

  1. A. Arterial pH 7.4
  2. B. Arterial PaO2 > 100 mmHg
  3. C. Serum osmolality >320 mOsmol/Kg
  4. D. Arterial PaCO2 < 35 mmHg

Answer: C. Mannitol works by drawing water out; above 320 mOsm/kg it risks kidney damage, so it is stopped.

Head injury management

AskedRepeated46 past questions

Extradural haematoma: middle meningeal artery, lucid interval, biconvex (lens) shape on CT. Subdural: bridging veins, crescent shape, elderly and alcoholics. GCS: eyes 4, verbal 5, motor 6; 8 or less means intubate. Raccoon eyes and Battle sign mean a base of skull fracture.

  4. D. Congenital skull fracture

Answer: A. Round holes in the skull after treatment for raised pressure are burr holes from earlier surgery.

Oncology Surgery 56 questions

TNM staging

Asked18 past questions

T = the primary tumour's size and spread, N = nodes, M = distant metastasis. Breast T4: chest wall (4a), skin ulcer, peau d'orange or satellite nodules (4b), both (4c), inflammatory carcinoma (4d). Stage IV always means distant spread.

Transplant & ICU Concepts 9 questions

Organ preservation

Asked3 past questions

Cold storage at about 4 °C in University of Wisconsin solution. Safe cold times: heart about 4-6 hours, liver about 12, kidney up to 24-36. Reperfusion syndrome in liver transplant comes when the portal vein clamp is released.

TPN complications

Asked3 past questions

Enteral feeding is preferred whenever the gut works. TPN goes through a central line; complications: line sepsis, hyperglycaemia, liver dysfunction, refeeding syndrome (low phosphate, potassium, magnesium).
