Neonatology 105 questions

Neonatal resuscitation

Must-doRepeated23 past questions

Term babies start in room air (21% oxygen). Heart rate under 100: ventilate. Under 60 after 30 seconds of good ventilation: chest compressions at 3:1 with breaths, and 100% oxygen. Still under 60: IV adrenaline.

  1. A. Continue ventilation alone
  2. B. Administer epinephrine immediately
  3. C. Provide oxygen by hood
  4. D. Start chest compressions with ventilation

Answer: D. A heart rate under 60 after 30 seconds of effective ventilation needs chest compressions with ventilation.

Birth asphyxia and HIE staging

Must-doRepeated5 past questions

Sarnat staging of hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy: stage 1 hyperalert, stage 2 lethargic with seizures, stage 3 stupor and flaccid. Therapeutic cooling within 6 hours for moderate to severe HIE in babies of 36 weeks or more.

Prematurity and low birth weight

Must-doRepeated10 past questions

Preterm under 37 weeks; low birth weight under 2.5 kg, very low under 1.5 kg, extremely low under 1 kg. Kangaroo mother care for stable small babies. Day-one fluids for a preterm baby about 80 mL/kg, rising daily. Watch for hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, RDS, NEC, retinopathy.

Neonatal jaundice

Must-doRepeated19 past questions

Jaundice in the first 24 hours is always pathological (haemolysis: Rh, ABO). Physiological jaundice appears after 24 hours and fades in 1-2 weeks. Prolonged jaundice with pale stools and dark urine is cholestasis: rule out biliary atresia early (Kasai operation before 60 days).

  3. C. Biliary atresia
  4. D. Gilbert syndrome

Answer: C. Pale stools with ductular proliferation and fibrosis on biopsy point to biliary atresia.

Neonatal sepsis

Must-doRepeated9 past questions

Early onset (under 72 hours): from the mother, mainly E. coli, group B strep, Listeria. Late onset: hospital organisms like Klebsiella and Staph. Blood culture is the gold standard. Empirical ampicillin with gentamicin.

  3. C. Enterococcus faecalis
  4. D. Corynebacterium diphtheriae

Answer: A. A small Gram-positive rod that grows in the cold and causes newborn meningitis is Listeria.

RDS

Must-doRepeated12 past questions

Surfactant deficiency in preterm babies. X-ray: ground glass with air bronchograms. Prevented by antenatal steroids to the mother; treated with CPAP and surfactant. Transient tachypnoea (fluid in lungs) is common after caesarean birth and settles in a day or two.

  1. A. Meconium aspiration
  2. B. Surfactant deficiency
  3. C. Transient fluid retention
  4. D. Bacterial pneumonia

Answer: B. Ground-glass lungs with air bronchograms in a preterm baby are respiratory distress syndrome from surfactant deficiency.

Nutrition 82 questions

PEM: marasmus, kwashiorkor, SAM management

Must-doRepeated33 past questions

Marasmus: severe wasting, no oedema. Kwashiorkor: oedema, skin and hair changes, fatty liver. SAM: weight-for-height under −3 SD, MUAC under 11.5 cm, or bilateral pitting oedema. Treat the 10 steps: hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, dehydration (ReSoMal) first; F-75 then F-100. Shock: RL with 5% dextrose, or half-normal saline with 5% dextrose, slowly.

Try a past question

  1. A. Normal saline
  2. B. Ringer’s lactate
  3. C. 5% Dextrose with half-normal saline
  4. D. 5% Dextrose alone

Answer: C. In SAM with shock, give a dextrose-containing fluid (5% dextrose with half-normal saline) slowly, to avoid overload.

Vitamin deficiencies

Must-doRepeated24 past questions

Vitamin A: night blindness, Bitot spots, keratomalacia; supplements from 9 months, every 6 months to age 5. Vitamin D: rickets (widened wrists, rachitic rosary, bow legs; high ALP). Vitamin K at birth prevents newborn bleeding. Measles: give vitamin A on 2 days.

Breastfeeding and complementary feeding

Must-doRepeated15 past questions

Start breastfeeding within an hour of birth; exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then complementary foods with breastfeeding to 2 years. Colostrum is rich in IgA. Breastfeeding is contraindicated in galactosaemia; continue it in hepatitis B (with vaccine and HBIG) and in HIV on ART.

Growth & Development 64 questions

Developmental milestones

Must-doRepeatedINI-CET24 past questions

Social smile 6 weeks; head holding 3 months; sits without support 6-8 months; stands with support 9 months; walks alone 12-15 months; first words 12 months; 2-word phrases 2 years. Denver II is a screening test, not a diagnosis. Red flag: no sitting by 9 months, no walking by 18 months.

Try a past question

  1. A. Bayley Scales of Infant Development II
  2. B. Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children IV
  3. C. Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scale II
  4. D. Denver II

Answer: D. Denver II only screens; Bayley and Wechsler are definitive assessments.

Growth: head circumference, height velocity, bone age, growth charts

Must-doRepeated30 past questions

Birth weight doubles by 5 months and triples by 1 year. Head circumference 35 cm at birth, about 45 cm at 1 year. Anterior fontanelle closes by 18 months (range 12-24). Constitutional delay: short with delayed bone age and normal growth rate; familial short stature: bone age normal.

Try a past question

  1. A. Constitutional growth delay
  2. B. Familial short stature
  3. C. Growth hormone deficiency
  4. D. Hypothyroidism

Answer: A. Short with a normal growth rate but delayed bone age is constitutional growth delay; they catch up late.

Paediatric Infectious Diseases 54 questions

Measles

High-yieldRepeated15 past questions

Prodrome: fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis; Koplik spots (bluish-white spots on the buccal mucosa) come before the rash, which starts behind the ears and face and spreads down. Complications: pneumonia (commonest cause of death), diarrhoea, SSPE years later. Give vitamin A.

  1. A. Mumps
  2. B. Measles
  3. C. Rubella
  4. D. Chicken pox

Answer: B. Koplik spots with fever, cough and red eyes are measles.

Diphtheria

High-yieldRepeated11 past questions

Corynebacterium diphtheriae: a grey adherent pseudomembrane that bleeds when removed, bull neck, myocarditis and nerve palsies from the toxin. Give antitoxin at once (do not wait for culture), plus penicillin or erythromycin. Culture on Loeffler or tellurite medium.

Pertussis

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

Bordetella pertussis: catarrhal stage (most infectious), then paroxysmal whooping cough with post-cough vomiting, then convalescence. Marked lymphocytosis. Azithromycin. Prevented by DPT.

Meningococcal meningitis

High-yieldRepeated5 past questions

Meningococcus: fever with a rapidly spreading purpuric rash and shock (Waterhouse-Friderichsen when the adrenals bleed). Give IV ceftriaxone or penicillin at once. Close contacts get rifampicin or ciprofloxacin.

  4. D. Neisseria meningitidis

Answer: D. High fever, a spreading purpuric rash and shock in a child point to meningococcal sepsis (Neisseria meningitidis).

Tuberculosis

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

Children rarely produce sputum: use gastric aspirate or induced sputum for CBNAAT. Contact with an adult case, weight loss and a long cough are the main clues. Same first-line drugs, dosed by weight; BCG protects mostly against severe forms (TB meningitis, miliary TB).

Dengue

Must-doRepeated3 past questions

Warning signs: belly pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, lethargy, a large liver, rising haematocrit with falling platelets. Shock comes as the fever settles (the critical phase, days 3-7). Treat with careful fluids; no NSAIDs or aspirin.

Genetics & Inborn Errors 50 questions

Galactosemia

Must-doRepeatedINI-CET9 past questions

Galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase deficiency: vomiting, jaundice and liver failure after starting milk, cataracts, E. coli sepsis. Reducing sugar in urine. Stop breast and cow's milk; lactose-free formula.

  3. C. galactosemia.
  4. D. HIV positive

Answer: C. In galactosaemia the baby cannot handle the galactose in breast milk, so breastfeeding must stop.

Phenylketonuria

Must-doRepeatedINI-CET3 past questions

Phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency: intellectual disability, fair skin, mousy (musty) odour, seizures. Found by newborn screening; a phenylalanine-restricted diet.

Down syndrome

Must-doRepeatedINI-CET10 past questions

Trisomy 21, mostly from non-disjunction (risk rises with maternal age). Upslanting eyes, flat nasal bridge, single palmar crease, sandal gap, hypotonia. Heart defect (endocardial cushion/AV canal), duodenal atresia, hypothyroidism, leukaemia, early Alzheimer.

  4. D. Noonan syndrome

Answer: B. Upslanting palpebral fissures, a flat nasal bridge and developmental delay are classic Down syndrome.

Turner syndrome

Must-doRepeatedINI-CET5 past questions

45,XO: short stature, webbed neck, widely spaced nipples, lymphoedema of hands and feet at birth, streak ovaries (primary amenorrhoea), coarctation of the aorta, horseshoe kidney. Growth hormone for height; oestrogen at puberty.

Paediatric Emergencies 50 questions

Fluid therapy

Must-doRepeatedINI-CET30 past questions

Maintenance (Holliday-Segar): 100 mL/kg for the first 10 kg, 50 mL/kg for the next 10, 20 mL/kg after that. Shock bolus: 20 mL/kg crystalloid (10 mL/kg in SAM or heart disease). Dehydration with diarrhoea: ORS for some dehydration; Ringer lactate IV for severe.

DKA

AskedINI-CET6 past questions

Children: fluids over 48 hours, insulin infusion at 0.05-0.1 unit/kg/hour started after the first hour of fluids, no insulin bolus, no routine bicarbonate. Cerebral oedema is the main danger: headache, slowing pulse, rising BP, falling consciousness; treat with mannitol or 3% saline.

  3. C. Insulin
  4. D. Thyroxine

Answer: C. DKA comes from insulin deficiency, which lets glucose rise and fat break down into ketones.

Status epilepticus

AskedINI-CET4 past questions

A seizure lasting 5 minutes or more. First: a benzodiazepine (IV lorazepam, or buccal or intranasal midazolam if there is no line). Then a second-line drug: levetiracetam, phenytoin or valproate. Check glucose.

  3. C. Phenobarbital
  4. D. Valproate

Answer: B. A benzodiazepine such as IV lorazepam is first-line for status epilepticus.

Nephrology 43 questions

Nephrotic syndrome

Must-doRepeated15 past questions

Heavy proteinuria (over 40 mg/m²/hour), low albumin, oedema, high cholesterol. In children usually minimal change disease: steroid-sensitive, no biopsy needed first. Complications: infections (peritonitis), clots.

PSGN

High-yieldRepeated13 past questions

Haematuria (cola-coloured urine), oedema and hypertension 1-2 weeks after a strep throat (3-6 weeks after skin infection). Low C3 that returns to normal in 6-8 weeks; raised ASO. Supportive treatment: salt and fluid limits, diuretics.

  3. C. Severe hypoalbuminaemia
  4. D. Positive ANA

Answer: B. Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis consumes complement, so C3 is low.

HUS

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

A triad: haemolytic anaemia with fragmented red cells, low platelets and acute kidney injury, usually after bloody diarrhoea from Shiga-toxin E. coli (O157:H7). Supportive care and dialysis; antibiotics are usually avoided.

Pediatric Cardiology 37 questions

TOF

Must-doRepeated8 past questions

Four parts: VSD, pulmonary stenosis, overriding aorta, right ventricular hypertrophy. The commonest cyanotic heart disease after infancy. Boot-shaped heart on X-ray. Tet spells: knee-chest position, oxygen, morphine, propranolol. Squatting relieves it.

VSD

Must-doRepeated8 past questions

The commonest congenital heart defect overall. Pansystolic murmur at the left lower sternal border. Many small muscular VSDs close by themselves. A large one causes heart failure in infancy and, if untreated, Eisenmenger syndrome.

PDA

High-yieldRepeated11 past questions

Continuous machinery murmur with bounding pulses; common in preterm babies. Closed with indomethacin, ibuprofen or paracetamol. Kept open with prostaglandin E1 in duct-dependent lesions (like transposition).

  4. D. Coarctation of aorta

Answer: A. A continuous machinery murmur with bounding pulses in a preterm baby is a patent ductus arteriosus.

Coarctation

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

Weak or delayed femoral pulses, BP higher in the arms than legs. Rib notching and the figure-3 sign on X-ray in older children. Linked to Turner syndrome and a bicuspid aortic valve.

  4. D. Ebstein anomaly

Answer: B. A pulse difference between arms and legs is the classic sign of coarctation of the aorta.

Paediatric Neurology 36 questions

West syndrome / infantile spasms

Must-do7 past questions

Infantile spasms in the first year: clusters of flexor spasms, developmental regression, and hypsarrhythmia on EEG. Treat with ACTH or oral steroids; vigabatrin first if tuberous sclerosis is the cause.

  1. A. West syndrome
  2. B. Febrile seizure
  3. C. Lennox-Gastaut syndrome
  4. D. Benign myoclonic epilepsy

Answer: A. Clusters of flexor spasms, regression and hypsarrhythmia make up West syndrome.

Cerebral palsy

High-yield4 past questions

A non-progressive brain injury before, during or soon after birth (prematurity and asphyxia are common causes). Spastic CP is the commonest type; spastic diplegia is typical of preterm babies (periventricular leukomalacia). Managed with physiotherapy and a team approach.

Meningitis

High-yield11 past questions

Bacterial meningitis after the newborn period: pneumococcus, meningococcus, Hib. CSF: high neutrophils, high protein, low sugar. TB meningitis: lymphocytes, very high protein, low sugar, slow onset. Ceftriaxone empirically; steroids reduce deafness in Hib.

Neurocutaneous syndromes

High-yieldINI-CET6 past questions

Tuberous sclerosis: ash-leaf macules, adenoma sebaceum, shagreen patch, infantile spasms, cardiac rhabdomyoma. NF1: café-au-lait spots, axillary freckles, Lisch nodules. Sturge-Weber: port-wine stain on the face, seizures, tram-track calcification.

Haematology 30 questions

Iron deficiency anaemia

Must-doRepeated6 past questions

The commonest anaemia in Indian children. Microcytic, hypochromic, raised RDW, low ferritin. Mentzer index (MCV/RBC count) over 13 favours iron deficiency, under 13 thalassaemia trait. Oral iron 3 mg/kg/day; continue for months after Hb recovers.

  2. B. Iron deficiency anemia
  3. C. Anemia of chronic disease
  4. D. Sideroblastic anemia

Answer: B. Microcytic hypochromic anaemia with a raised RDW in a toddler is iron deficiency.

Thalassemia

Must-doRepeated7 past questions

β-thalassaemia major shows at 6-9 months as fetal haemoglobin falls: severe anaemia, a big spleen, chipmunk face, hair-on-end skull X-ray. HbF raised on electrophoresis. Regular transfusions plus iron chelation (deferoxamine, deferasirox, deferiprone); stem cell transplant cures.

  3. C. Dimercaprol
  4. D. EDTA

Answer: B. Deferoxamine binds the extra iron that builds up from repeated transfusions.

ITP

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

Low platelets alone, after a viral illness, in an otherwise well child; normal Hb and white count, normal smear apart from big platelets. Most recover on their own; treat bleeding with steroids or IVIG.

  3. C. Disseminated intravascular coagulation
  4. D. Immune thrombocytopenic purpura

Answer: D. Isolated low platelets with normal Hb and white count in a well child is immune thrombocytopenic purpura.

Leukaemia

High-yieldRepeated4 past questions

ALL is the commonest childhood cancer, peak at 2-5 years: pallor, fever, bleeding, bone pain, a big liver and spleen. Good outlook: age 1-10, white count under 50,000, hyperdiploidy, t(12;21).

Endocrinology 27 questions

Congenital hypothyroidism

Must-doINI-CET8 past questions

The commonest preventable cause of intellectual disability; found by newborn TSH screening. Signs: prolonged jaundice, large tongue, hoarse cry, constipation, umbilical hernia, large anterior fontanelle, dry skin. Start levothyroxine as early as possible.

CAH

AskedINI-CET7 past questions

21-hydroxylase deficiency is the commonest form: ambiguous genitalia in girls; salt-wasting crisis in the first 1-2 weeks with low sodium, high potassium and vomiting. Raised 17-hydroxyprogesterone. Hydrocortisone plus fludrocortisone.

Precocious and delayed puberty

AskedINI-CET8 past questions

Girls: breast budding (thelarche) first, then pubic hair, the growth spurt, and menarche about 2 years after thelarche. Boys: testicular enlargement first. Precocious puberty: before 8 years in girls, 9 in boys. Delayed: no signs by 13 in girls, 14 in boys.

Vaccination 13 questions

UIP schedule

Must-do8 past questions

India's schedule: at birth BCG, OPV-0, hepatitis B birth dose; 6, 10, 14 weeks pentavalent, OPV, rotavirus; fractional IPV at 6 and 14 weeks; PCV at 6 and 14 weeks; 9 months measles-rubella (MR-1), vitamin A, PCV booster, third fIPV; 16-24 months MR-2, DPT booster 1, OPV booster; 5-6 years DPT booster 2; 10 and 16 years Td.

Catch-up immunization

Must-do3 past questions

A missed dose is never restarted; continue from where it stopped. Keep the minimum 4-week gap between doses of the same vaccine. BCG can be given up to 1 year; the pentavalent vaccine up to 1 year.

Respiratory Disorders 25 questions

Bronchiolitis

AskedINI-CET3 past questions

Infants under 2 years, RSV the commonest cause: cough, wheeze, crackles, breathing difficulty. Supportive care: oxygen, fluids, nasal suction. Bronchodilators and steroids do not help routinely.

Asthma

AskedINI-CET5 past questions

Acute attack: salbutamol by spacer or nebuliser, oral or IV steroids, oxygen; add ipratropium and magnesium sulfate in severe attacks. Signs of a life-threatening attack: silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion, confusion, SpO2 under 92%.

Pneumonia

AskedINI-CET7 past questions

The commonest cause of death in children under 5 worldwide. Fast breathing by age: 60 or more under 2 months, 50 or more at 2-12 months, 40 or more at 1-5 years. Chest indrawing means severe pneumonia. Amoxicillin for community pneumonia. In cystic fibrosis, young children get Staph aureus, later Pseudomonas.

Croup

AskedINI-CET8 past questions

Parainfluenza virus, 6 months to 3 years: barking cough, hoarse voice, stridor worse at night; steeple sign on X-ray. Oral dexamethasone; nebulised adrenaline if severe. Epiglottitis (Hib): toxic, drooling, tripod position, thumb sign; secure the airway first.
