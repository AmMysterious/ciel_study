Endocrinology 261 questions

Diabetes mellitus: DKA, HHS, insulin, drugs, complications

Must-doRepeated68 past questions

Diagnosis: fasting 126 mg/dL or more, 2-hour 200 or more, HbA1c 6.5% or more. DKA: glucose, ketones, acidosis; treat with fluids first, then insulin infusion, and watch potassium (give it once it is under 5.3). HHS: very high glucose and osmolality without much ketosis. HbA1c shows control over about 3 months.

Try a past question

  2. B. Random Blood Sugar
  3. C. Serum fructosemine
  4. D. HbA 1c

Answer: D. HbA1c reflects average glucose over the red cell's lifespan, about 3 months.

Thyroid

Must-doRepeated68 past questions

Hyperthyroid: Graves (diffuse goitre, eye signs, TSH-receptor antibodies). Hypothyroid: Hashimoto is the commonest cause where iodine is adequate. TSH is the best screening test. Propylthiouracil in the first trimester, carbimazole otherwise. Thyroid storm: β-blocker, PTU, then iodine an hour later, plus steroids.

Pituitary: acromegaly, prolactinoma, diabetes insipidus

Must-do49 past questions

Prolactinoma: the commonest pituitary tumour; cabergoline first. Acromegaly: screen with IGF-1, confirm by failure of GH suppression after a glucose load. Diabetes insipidus: dilute urine; the water deprivation test, then desmopressin separates central from nephrogenic. SIADH: low sodium with concentrated urine.

Adrenal: Addison, Cushing, pheochromocytoma

Must-do60 past questions

Addison: low sodium, high potassium, hypotension, pigmentation; adrenal crisis is treated with IV hydrocortisone and saline. Cushing: the commonest cause is steroid medicine; of the internal causes, a pituitary adenoma (Cushing disease). Screen with the overnight dexamethasone test or 24-hour urine cortisol. Conn: hypertension with low potassium.

Cardiology 223 questions

ECG: heart blocks, arrhythmias, MI localization, potassium changes

Must-doRepeated91 past questions

Heart blocks: first degree PR over 200 ms; Mobitz I PR lengthens then a beat drops; Mobitz II sudden drop (pacemaker); complete block, P and QRS unrelated. Infarct site: II, III, aVF inferior (RCA); V1-V4 anterior (LAD); I, aVL, V5-V6 lateral. Hyperkalaemia: tall tented T, then wide QRS. AF: anticoagulate if CHA2DS2-VASc is 2 or more in men (3 in women).

Answer: A. A CHA2DS2-VASc score of 3 means a high stroke risk: start oral anticoagulation.

Acute coronary syndrome

Must-doRepeated36 past questions

STEMI: primary PCI within 90 minutes, or thrombolysis within 12 hours if PCI is not available. Give aspirin, a P2Y12 blocker, heparin and a statin. Troponin is the marker of choice. Wellens pattern (deep T inversion in V2-V3) warns of critical proximal LAD narrowing.

  3. C. Acute Pericarditis

Answer: B. Deep T inversion in V2-V3 between pains is Wellens syndrome: a critical proximal LAD stenosis.

Heart failure

Must-do33 past questions

HFrEF: ACE inhibitor or ARB or ARNI, β-blocker, spironolactone and an SGLT2 inhibitor reduce deaths; diuretics only ease symptoms. BNP or NT-proBNP helps diagnosis. Acute pulmonary oedema: sit up, oxygen, IV furosemide, nitrates.

Valvular diseases and murmurs

Must-doRepeated47 past questions

Murmurs: aortic stenosis, ejection systolic to the carotids; mitral regurgitation, pansystolic to the axilla; mitral stenosis, mid-diastolic rumble with opening snap. Valsalva and standing make HOCM and mitral valve prolapse louder; almost everything else gets softer. Rheumatic fever is the commonest cause of mitral stenosis in India.

  2. B. Mitral regurgitation
  3. C. VSD
  4. D. HOCM

Answer: D. Valsalva reduces venous return, the ventricle gets smaller and the HOCM obstruction gets worse, so the murmur is louder.

JVP waveforms

Must-doRepeated17 past questions

a wave = atrial contraction: absent in AF, large in tricuspid stenosis and pulmonary hypertension, cannon a waves in complete heart block. Large v wave in tricuspid regurgitation. Steep y descent in constrictive pericarditis; Kussmaul sign (JVP rises on breathing in) in constriction.

  4. D. Steep y descent

Answer: A. In atrial fibrillation the atria do not contract, so the a wave disappears.

Neurology 193 questions

Stroke: syndromes, localization, thrombolysis

Must-doRepeated57 past questions

Anterior cerebral artery: leg weakness more than arm. Middle cerebral: face and arm, aphasia if dominant side. Posterior cerebral: vision loss. Thrombolysis with alteplase within 4.5 hours once a CT has ruled out bleeding. Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome: PICA or vertebral artery.

Cranial nerves

Must-doRepeated17 past questions

Trigeminal neuralgia: brief electric pain on touching the face; carbamazepine first. Bell palsy (lower motor neurone facial palsy): steroids within 72 hours; the forehead is weak too, unlike a stroke. Third nerve palsy with a blown pupil = compression (posterior communicating aneurysm).

Myasthenia gravis and Guillain-Barré syndrome

Must-doRepeated37 past questions

Nephrology 170 questions

AKI

Must-do12 past questions

Prerenal: urine sodium under 20, FENa under 1%, bland urine. Acute tubular necrosis: muddy brown granular casts, FENa over 2%. Red cell casts mean glomerulonephritis; white cell casts mean pyelonephritis or interstitial nephritis. Urgent dialysis for refractory potassium, acidosis, fluid overload, uraemic signs.

  4. D. Glomerulonephritis

Answer: C. Muddy brown granular casts after a long low-BP spell are acute tubular necrosis.

CKD

Must-do18 past questions

Staged by GFR (and albuminuria). Diabetes and hypertension are the commonest causes. Anaemia from low erythropoietin; renal bone disease from high phosphate and low vitamin D (secondary hyperparathyroidism). ACE inhibitors or ARBs slow proteinuric disease.

  4. D. Ferritin

Answer: B. Damaged kidneys make less erythropoietin, so the marrow makes fewer red cells.

Electrolytes: sodium and potassium disorders

Must-doRepeated81 past questions

Hyperkalaemia: first calcium gluconate to protect the heart, then insulin with glucose and salbutamol to shift potassium in, then remove it (binders, dialysis). Hyponatraemia: correct slowly (no more than about 8-10 mmol/L in 24 hours) or osmotic demyelination follows.

Try a past question

  1. A. Insulin
  2. B. Sodium bicarbonate
  3. C. Furosemide
  4. D. Calcium gluconate

Answer: D. Calcium gluconate stabilises the heart muscle membrane at once; it does not lower potassium.

Acid-base disorders and ABG

Must-doRepeated47 past questions

Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO3), normal about 12. High-gap acidosis: ketones, lactate, renal failure, methanol, ethylene glycol, salicylates. Normal-gap: diarrhoea, renal tubular acidosis. RTA type 4 (aldosterone lack) has high potassium; types 1 and 2 have low potassium. Winter's formula checks respiratory compensation.

Try a past question

  2. B. Type 2 (Proximal)
  3. C. Type 4
  4. D. All types

Answer: C. Type 4 RTA comes from low aldosterone action, so potassium is not excreted.

Respiratory Medicine 146 questions

Asthma and COPD

Must-doRepeated40 past questions

Asthma: reversible obstruction (FEV1 rises 12% and 200 mL after a bronchodilator). COPD: FEV1/FVC under 0.70 after a bronchodilator. In COPD with CO2 retention, aim oxygen saturation at 88-92%. Stopping smoking and long-term oxygen are what improve survival.

  3. C. 88-92%
  4. D. 70-80%

Answer: C. Too much oxygen in COPD can raise CO2; the target saturation is 88-92%.

ILD and sarcoidosis

Must-do24 past questions

Pleural diseases

Must-do22 past questions

Gastroenterology 118 questions

Cirrhosis, portal hypertension, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy

High-yieldRepeated60 past questions

Portal hypertension: varices, splenomegaly, ascites. Child with bleeding varices and a big spleen but normal liver function and no ascites: extrahepatic portal vein obstruction. Hepatitis B and C

High-yield24 past questions

HBsAg = current infection. Anti-HBs alone = vaccinated. Anti-HBs with anti-HBc IgG = past infection, immune. IgM anti-HBc = acute infection (and the window period). HBeAg = high infectivity. Hepatitis C is now cured with direct-acting antivirals (sofosbuvir-based).

Vasculitis

High-yield41 past questions

Large vessel: giant cell arteritis (older, headache, jaw pain; start steroids at once to save sight), Takayasu (young women, absent pulses). Tropical fevers: malaria, dengue, leptospirosis, scrub typhus, enteric fever

High-yieldRepeated50 past questions

Sepsis

High-yield7 past questions

Sepsis = infection with organ dysfunction (SOFA up 2 or more). Septic shock: vasopressors needed for MAP 65 or more, and lactate over 2 despite fluids. Give antibiotics within the first hour, 30 mL/kg crystalloid, and noradrenaline as the first vasopressor. Gram-negative endotoxin is lipopolysaccharide.

ICU scores: SOFA, qSOFA

AskedINI-CET3 past questions

qSOFA (one point each): breathing rate 22 or more, systolic BP 100 or less, altered mentation (GCS under 15). Two or more suggests a high risk of death from sepsis. Lactate is not part of qSOFA.

Toxicology 29 questions

Organophosphorus poisoning

AskedRepeatedINI-CET6 past questions

Organophosphates: pinpoint pupils, sweating, secretions, slow pulse, fasciculations. Atropine titrated to dry chest secretions, plus pralidoxime early. Watch for the intermediate syndrome (neck and breathing muscle weakness) after 1-4 days.

Methanol: blurred or lost vision, high anion gap acidosis. Ethylene glycol: kidney failure with calcium oxalate crystals. Both are treated with fomepizole (or ethanol) and haemodialysis.
