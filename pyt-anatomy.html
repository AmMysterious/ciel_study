Head & Neck Anatomy 154 questions

Cranial nerves

Must-doRepeated82 past questions

Corneal reflex: sensory V1 (trigeminal), motor VII. Taste: anterior two-thirds by chorda tympani (VII), posterior third by IX. Gag reflex: IX in, X out. Parasympathetic: III (eye), VII (lacrimal, submandibular, sublingual), IX (parotid), X (thorax and abdomen). Trochlear is the only nerve leaving the back of the brainstem.

Try a past question

Which cranial nerve is responsible for corneal reflex sensation?

  1. A. Trigeminal nerve
  2. B. Optic nerve
  3. C. Facial nerve
  4. D. Oculomotor nerve

Answer: A. The cornea's sensation comes from the ophthalmic division of the trigeminal nerve.

Cavernous sinus

Must-doRepeated10 past questions

Inside the cavernous sinus: internal carotid artery and VI nerve; in its lateral wall: III, IV, V1, V2. Spread of infection from the 'danger area' of the face. Carotid-cavernous fistula: pulsating proptosis, chemosis, an orbital bruit, often after head injury.

  2. B. Cavernous sinus thrombosis
  3. C. Carotid-cavernous fistula
  4. D. Thyroid eye disease

Answer: C. Pulsatile proptosis with chemosis and a bruit is a carotid-cavernous fistula.

Scalp

Must-do7 past questions

Layers: Skin, Connective tissue, Aponeurosis, Loose areolar tissue (the 'danger layer', emissary veins), Pericranium. Bleeding from scalp wounds is heavy because vessels are held open by dense tissue. The back of the scalp is supplied by the greater occipital nerve (C2).

  2. B. Lesser occipital nerve
  3. C. Auriculotemporal nerve
  4. D. Supraorbital nerve

Answer: A. The greater occipital nerve (C2) supplies the back of the scalp.

Parotid gland and facial nerve

Must-doRepeated16 past questions

Parasympathetic supply: glossopharyngeal nerve (lesser petrosal) via the otic ganglion to the auriculotemporal nerve. The facial nerve divides inside the gland into 5 branches but does not supply it. Stensen duct opens opposite the upper second molar.

Answer: C. The parotid's secretory fibres come from the glossopharyngeal nerve through the otic ganglion.

Pharyngeal arches

Must-do4 past questions

The 1st arch gives the muscles of chewing, the 2nd the muscles of facial expression, the 3rd stylopharyngeus, the 4th and 6th the laryngeal and pharyngeal muscles. Pouches 3 and 4 form the parathyroids; their failure causes low calcium in newborns (DiGeorge).

  2. B. 2,3
  3. C. 3,4
  4. D. 4,6

Answer: C. The parathyroids come from the 3rd and 4th pouches; their failure gives neonatal hypocalcaemia.

Larynx

Must-doRepeated25 past questions

All intrinsic laryngeal muscles are supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve except the cricothyroid (external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve). Posterior cricoarytenoid is the only muscle that opens the cords. Above the cords, sensation is by the internal laryngeal nerve; below, by the recurrent.

  2. B. Lateral cricoarytenoid
  3. C. Interarytenoid
  4. D. Cricothyroid

Answer: D. The superior laryngeal nerve's external branch supplies the cricothyroid.

Orbit and extraocular muscles

Must-do13 past questions

Lateral wall: zygomatic bone and greater wing of sphenoid (the strongest). Medial wall: thin lamina papyracea of the ethmoid (infection spreads into the orbit). Floor: maxilla (blow-out fractures). LR6 SO4: lateral rectus by VI, superior oblique by IV, the rest by III.

Embryology 95 questions

Pharyngeal apparatus

Must-doRepeated22 past questions

Pouches: 1st, middle ear and auditory tube; 2nd, tonsil; 3rd, inferior parathyroids and thymus; 4th, superior parathyroids and ultimobranchial body. DiGeorge syndrome (3rd and 4th pouches): low calcium and no thymus. Arches: 1st (V), 2nd (VII), 3rd (IX), 4th and 6th (X).

  3. C. Parathyroid glands and thymus
  4. D. Palatine tonsil

Answer: C. The third pharyngeal pouch forms the inferior parathyroids and the thymus.

Heart development

Must-doRepeated13 past questions

Fetal shunts: ductus venosus (bypasses the liver to the IVC), foramen ovale (right to left atrium), ductus arteriosus (pulmonary trunk to aorta). After birth they become the ligamentum venosum, fossa ovalis and ligamentum arteriosum. Septum defects and tetralogy come from abnormal septation.

Try a past question

  1. A. Umbilical vein - liver
  2. B. Right atrium – left atrium
  3. C. By passing liver to IVC
  4. D. Pulmonary artery - aorta

Answer: C. The ductus venosus carries placental blood past the liver straight to the IVC.

Neural tube

High-yieldRepeated7 past questions

The neural tube closes by day 28; the front (rostral) end closes around day 25, the back end around day 27-28. Failure at the front: anencephaly; at the back: spina bifida. Folic acid before conception prevents most cases; raised AFP in maternal blood and amniotic fluid.

Try a past question

  2. B. Rachischisis
  3. C. Anencephaly
  4. D. Spina bifida

Answer: C. Failure of the rostral neuropore to close causes anencephaly.

Gastrointestinal development

High-yield18 past questions

The dorsal pancreatic bud forms the head (upper part), neck, body and tail; the ventral bud forms the lower head and uncinate process and the main duct. Annular pancreas: ventral bud wraps round the duodenum. Meckel diverticulum: rule of 2s, remnant of the vitello-intestinal duct.

Try a past question

  1. A. Head
  2. B. Neck, body and tail
  3. C. Uncinate process
  4. D. Main pancreatic duct

Answer: B. The dorsal bud forms the neck, body and tail of the pancreas.

Urogenital development

High-yield25 past questions

The ureteric bud forms the ureter, pelvis, calyces and collecting ducts; the metanephric mesoderm forms nephrons. Failure of the ureteric bud = renal agenesis. Horseshoe kidney is trapped under the inferior mesenteric artery. Müllerian ducts form the uterus and tubes; Wolffian ducts the male ducts.

Try a past question

A newborn presents with an absent left kidney. Which embryonic structure failed to develop?

  1. A. Metanephric blastema
  2. B. Pronephros
  3. C. Mesonephros
  4. D. Ureteric bud

Answer: D. Without the ureteric bud the kidney never forms: renal agenesis.

Neuroanatomy 83 questions

Spinal cord: tracts and cord syndromes

Must-doRepeated17 past questions

Blood supply: one anterior spinal artery (front two-thirds) and two posterior spinal arteries. The cord ends at L1-L2 in adults (L3 in newborns). Brown-Séquard: same-side weakness and loss of position sense, opposite-side loss of pain and temperature. Anterior cord syndrome spares dorsal columns.

Try a past question

Which of the following is the primary arterial supply of the spinal cord?

  1. A. Posterior spinal arteries
  2. B. Anterior spinal artery
  3. C. Vertebral artery
  4. D. Radicular arteries

Answer: B. The anterior spinal artery supplies the front two-thirds of the cord, the larger part.

Brainstem and cranial nerve nuclei

Must-doRepeated22 past questions

Midbrain: III and IV nuclei, red nucleus, substantia nigra. Pons: V, VI, VII, VIII. Medulla: IX, X, XI, XII. Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome: PICA, crossed sensory loss, hoarseness, Horner. Medial medullary: XII palsy with opposite hemiplegia.

Which part of the brainstem contains the red nucleus?

  1. A. Midbrain
  2. B. Medulla
  3. C. Pons
  4. D. Thalamus

Answer: A. The red nucleus lies in the midbrain.

Internal capsule

Must-doRepeated7 past questions

The posterior limb carries the corticospinal fibres to the body; the genu carries corticobulbar fibres. A small bleed or lacunar infarct here (lenticulostriate arteries) causes complete hemiplegia on the opposite side, face, arm and leg equally.

Cerebellum

Must-do10 past questions

The cerebellum controls balance and coordination on the same side. Vermis: trunk and gait; hemispheres: limbs. Inferior cerebellar peduncle carries input from the spinal cord; superior peduncle carries output. Signs of damage: ataxia, intention tremor, nystagmus.

Try a past question

Which part of the brain controls balance and coordination?

  1. A. Basal ganglia
  2. B. Cerebellum
  3. C. Cerebral cortex
  4. D. Brainstem

Answer: B. The cerebellum coordinates movement and balance.

Basal ganglia

Must-do6 past questions

The basal ganglia are the caudate, putamen and globus pallidus (with subthalamic nucleus and substantia nigra in the circuit). Striatum = caudate + putamen. Lentiform = putamen + globus pallidus. Damage gives movement disorders (Parkinson, chorea, hemiballismus).

Blood supply of the brain

Must-doRepeated17 past questions

Middle cerebral artery: lateral surface of the hemisphere (face and arm motor and sensory areas, speech). Anterior cerebral: medial surface (leg, paracentral lobule). Posterior cerebral: occipital lobe (vision). Lenticulostriate branches of the MCA supply the internal capsule; they bleed in hypertension.

Upper Limb 76 questions

Brachial plexus

Must-doRepeated15 past questions

Roots C5-T1. Upper trunk injury (Erb palsy, C5-C6): waiter's tip position, after a difficult birth or a fall on the shoulder. Lower trunk (Klumpke, C8-T1): claw hand, possibly Horner syndrome. Musculocutaneous, median, ulnar, radial and axillary are the main terminal branches.

Nerve injuries

Must-doRepeated42 past questions

Winged scapula: long thoracic nerve (serratus anterior). Wrist drop: radial nerve (spiral groove, humerus shaft fracture). Claw hand: ulnar (medial epicondyle). Ape thumb and loss of pinch: median (carpal tunnel, supracondylar fracture). Loss of shoulder abduction after neck of humerus fracture: axillary nerve.

  4. D. Levator scapulae

Answer: B. Winging of the scapula comes from serratus anterior paralysis (long thoracic nerve).

Shoulder joint

Must-do15 past questions

The most mobile and most often dislocated joint (anterior dislocation; risk to the axillary nerve). Rotator cuff: supraspinatus (starts abduction, most often torn), infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis. Sprengel deformity: a high, small scapula that limits abduction.

  3. C. Congenital torticollis
  4. D. Cervical Dystonia

Answer: B. An undescended, high scapula (Sprengel deformity) limits shoulder abduction in a child.

Carpal tunnel

Must-do3 past questions

Through the carpal tunnel: the median nerve and 9 tendons (4 FDS, 4 FDP, FPL). Carpal tunnel syndrome: tingling of the thumb, index and middle fingers, worse at night, thenar wasting; Tinel and Phalen tests. Common in pregnancy, hypothyroidism, diabetes.

Abdomen 75 questions

Inguinal canal

Must-doRepeated20 past questions

The deep ring is a gap in the transversalis fascia, lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels; the superficial ring is in the external oblique. Contents: spermatic cord in males (round ligament in females), ilioinguinal nerve. Hesselbach triangle (direct hernias): inferior epigastric vessels, lateral edge of rectus, inguinal ligament.

Try a past question

Which structure passes through the inguinal canal in males?

  1. A. Round ligament
  2. B. Spermatic cord
  3. C. Ilioinguinal nerve only
  4. D. Femoral nerve

Answer: B. In males the spermatic cord runs through the inguinal canal.

Portal vein and portosystemic anastomoses

Must-doRepeated6 past questions

The portal vein is formed behind the neck of the pancreas by the splenic and superior mesenteric veins. Portosystemic anastomoses (where varices form): lower oesophagus, around the umbilicus (caput medusae), rectum (anorectal), and retroperitoneum.

Try a past question

  1. A. Splenic and inferior mesenteric veins
  2. B. Splenic and superior mesenteric veins
  3. C. Superior and inferior mesenteric veins
  4. D. Left gastric and splenic veins

Answer: B. The splenic and superior mesenteric veins join behind the pancreatic neck to form the portal vein.

Peritoneum

High-yield16 past questions

Retroperitoneal organs (SAD PUCKER): suprarenals, aorta and IVC, duodenum (2nd to 4th parts), pancreas (except tail), ureters, colon (ascending and descending), kidneys, oesophagus, rectum. The epiploic foramen (Winslow) joins the greater and lesser sacs.

  1. A. Ascending colon

  3. C. Sigmoid colon
  4. D. Cecum

Answer: A. The ascending colon is retroperitoneal; the transverse and sigmoid colon have a mesentery.

Stomach

High-yield8 past questions

Lesser omentum runs from the lesser curvature to the liver; greater omentum hangs from the greater curvature. Arteries: left and right gastric along the lesser curve, gastroepiploics along the greater curve, short gastrics to the fundus.

Liver

High-yield14 past questions

Falciform ligament attaches the liver to the anterior abdominal wall and carries the ligamentum teres (old umbilical vein). The bare area touches the diaphragm. Couinaud divides the liver into 8 segments by portal and hepatic veins; the caudate lobe (segment I) drains directly into the IVC.

Thorax 56 questions

Heart: coronary arteries, conducting system

Must-doRepeated17 past questions

Right coronary artery supplies the SA node (about 60%) and AV node (about 80%, right dominance), inferior wall. Left anterior descending: front of the septum and anterior wall. Circumflex: lateral wall. The coronary sinus drains into the right atrium.

Mediastinum

High-yield15 past questions

The superior vena cava is formed by the two brachiocephalic veins; the azygos vein drains into it. Posterior mediastinum: oesophagus, descending aorta, thoracic duct, azygos veins, vagus nerves. Thymus in the anterior mediastinum.

Lungs: bronchopulmonary segments

Must-doRepeated6 past questions

The right lung has 10 segments in 3 lobes; the left about 8-10 in 2 lobes. Inhaled objects go to the right side (the right bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical): standing, to the posterior basal segment; lying on the back, to the superior segment of the lower lobe.

  4. D. Mediobasal segment

Answer: A. An inhaled peanut usually lodges in the right lower lobe, often its posterobasal segment.

Diaphragm

High-yieldRepeated14 past questions

Openings: IVC at T8 (with the right phrenic nerve), oesophagus at T10 (with the vagal trunks), aorta at T12 (with the thoracic duct and azygos). Phrenic nerve C3-C5. Oesophageal narrowings from the incisors: 15 cm (cricopharynx), 25 cm (aortic arch and left bronchus), 40 cm (diaphragm).

  2. B. Arch of the aorta
  3. C. Opening of the diaphragm
  4. D. Cricopharyngeal muscle

Answer: C. The constriction about 40 cm from the incisors is where the oesophagus passes through the diaphragm.

Lower Limb 52 questions

Femoral triangle

Must-do9 past questions

Femoral triangle: inguinal ligament, sartorius, adductor longus. Contents from lateral to medial: nerve, artery, vein, (empty space), lymphatics (NAVEL). The femoral nerve supplies quadriceps, sartorius and pectineus, not the adductors (obturator nerve).

  2. B. Rectus femoris
  3. C. Vastus medialis
  4. D. Adductor longus

Answer: D. Adductor longus is supplied by the obturator nerve, not the femoral nerve.

Sciatic nerve

Must-do3 past questions

The sciatic nerve (L4-S3), the largest nerve, leaves below the piriformis and divides into tibial and common peroneal nerves, usually above the knee. Injections in the wrong gluteal quadrant can damage it.

Gluteal region

Gluteus maximus: inferior gluteal nerve (extends the hip, climbing stairs). Gluteus medius and minimus: superior gluteal nerve (keep the pelvis level; Trendelenburg sign if weak). Safe injection site: the upper outer quadrant (to avoid the sciatic nerve).

Knee joint

Must-doRepeated12 past questions

ACL stops the tibia sliding forward (anterior drawer and Lachman tests); PCL stops it sliding back (posterior drawer). The medial meniscus is fixed to the medial ligament and torn more often. Unhappy triad: ACL, medial collateral and medial meniscus. Popliteus unlocks the knee.

  1. A. Anterior cruciate ligament

  3. C. Medial collateral ligament
  4. D. Lateral collateral ligament

Answer: B. The posterior cruciate ligament prevents backward movement of the tibia.

Nerve lesions: foot drop, Trendelenburg gait

Must-doRepeated21 past questions

Foot drop: common peroneal nerve, injured at the neck of the fibula. Deep peroneal: loss of feeling in the first web space. Trendelenburg sign (hip drops on the other side): superior gluteal nerve (gluteus medius and minimus). Tibial nerve: loss of plantar flexion, numb sole.

Epithelia

High-yield10 past questions

Simple squamous: alveoli, vessels (endothelium). Simple cuboidal with brush border: proximal tubule. Simple columnar: stomach, intestine. Pseudostratified ciliated: trachea. Transitional (urothelium): bladder. Stratified squamous: skin (keratinised), oesophagus (non-keratinised).

  3. C. Thin ascending limb
  4. D. Collecting duct

Answer: B. The proximal tubule is lined by cuboidal cells with a tall brush border (microvilli).

Adrenal cortex from outside in: zona glomerulosa (aldosterone), fasciculata (cortisol), reticularis (androgens); then the medulla (adrenaline). Exocrine glands: merocrine (most), apocrine (axillary sweat, breast fat), holocrine (sebaceous).

Skull and its foramina

Pelvic diaphragm

High-yield5 past questions

The pelvic diaphragm is the levator ani (pubococcygeus, iliococcygeus, puborectalis) and coccygeus. Puborectalis keeps faecal continence. Damage in childbirth leads to prolapse and incontinence.

Pudendal nerve

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

Pudendal nerve (S2-S4) leaves through the greater sciatic foramen and returns through the lesser, running in Alcock canal. A pudendal block is given near the ischial spine for childbirth. Supplies the perineum and the external sphincters.

Uterus

High-yieldRepeated5 past questions

The ureter passes under the uterine artery about 2 cm lateral to the cervix ('water under the bridge'), close to the lateral fornix: at risk in hysterectomy. Supports: cardinal (Mackenrodt) and uterosacral ligaments, and the levator ani.
