Ear 187 questions

CSOM (Safe vs Unsafe)

Must-doRepeated26 past questions

Safe (tubotympanic): central perforation, mucoid discharge, rarely dangerous. Unsafe (atticoantral): attic or marginal perforation, scanty foul-smelling discharge, cholesteatoma that erodes bone; complications include mastoiditis, brain abscess, facial palsy. Unsafe disease needs surgery (modified radical mastoidectomy).

Otosclerosis

Must-doRepeated20 past questions

Otosclerosis: young women, often worse in pregnancy, family history. Conductive loss with fixation of the stapes footplate; hears better in noise (paracusis Willisii); Carhart notch at 2 kHz on bone conduction; Schwartze sign (flamingo pink drum). Treat with stapedotomy or a hearing aid.

  2. B. Otosclerosis
  3. C. Noise-induced hearing loss
  4. D. Acoustic Neuroma

Answer: B. Hearing better in a noisy place (paracusis Willisii) is typical of otosclerosis.

Glomus tumor

Must-doRepeated4 past questions

Glomus tumour (paraganglioma): a red pulsating mass behind an intact eardrum, pulsatile tinnitus, conductive hearing loss. Brown sign: the mass blanches when ear canal pressure is raised. The commonest benign tumour of the middle ear; do not biopsy (it bleeds).

  3. C. chronic otitis media
  4. D. otosclerosis

Answer: A. A red bulging mass behind an intact drum with conductive hearing loss is a glomus tumour.

Hearing loss

Must-doRepeated77 past questions

Rinne positive (air better than bone) is normal or sensorineural; Rinne negative means conductive loss. Weber goes to the deaf ear in conductive loss and to the better ear in sensorineural loss. Noise-induced hearing loss: a notch at 4 kHz on the audiogram. Presbycusis: high frequencies lost first.

  2. B. Noise-induced hearing loss
  3. C. Presbyacusis
  4. D. Meniere's disease

Answer: B. A notch around 4 kHz on audiometry in a factory worker is noise-induced hearing loss.

Facial nerve palsy

Must-doRepeated16 past questions

Bell palsy: sudden lower motor neurone facial palsy, steroids within 72 hours. Ramsay Hunt (herpes zoster oticus): vesicles on the ear, pain, facial palsy; steroids with antivirals (valaciclovir). The commonest site of injury in temporal bone fracture and surgery is the tympanic and second genu parts.

Meniere’s disease

Must-doRepeated15 past questions

Ménière disease: endolymphatic hydrops. Episodes of vertigo, fluctuating low-frequency sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus and fullness in the ear. Glycerol test; electrocochleography. Treat with low salt, betahistine, diuretics; intratympanic gentamicin or surgery if severe.

Labyrinth & vestibular anatomy

Must-doRepeated31 past questions

Semicircular canals sense rotation; utricle and saccule sense linear movement. Caloric test, COWS: cold water gives nystagmus with the fast phase to the Opposite side, warm water to the Same side. BPPV: Dix-Hallpike test, Epley manoeuvre to treat (posterior canal is commonest).

Acute & chronic sinusitis

Must-doRepeated17 past questions

Maxillary sinus is the commonest affected in adults, ethmoid in children. Fungal sinusitis: allergic fungal sinusitis has thick 'peanut butter' mucin; invasive forms in the immunocompromised. CT is the investigation of choice; FESS for chronic disease.

Allergic rhinitis

High-yieldRepeated4 past questions

Allergic rhinitis: sneezing, watery discharge, itching, blocked nose; pale boggy mucosa. Type I hypersensitivity (IgE). Intranasal steroids are the most effective treatment; antihistamines and allergen avoidance help.

Deviated nasal septum (DNS)

High-yieldRepeated6 past questions

A deviated septum causes blockage on one side, sinusitis and nosebleeds. Septoplasty is preferred today (submucous resection removes more). Pressure from the middle turbinate on the septum can irritate the anterior ethmoidal nerve (Sluder's or contact point headache).

Nasal polyps (ethmoidal vs antrochoanal)

High-yieldRepeated11 past questions

Ethmoidal polyps: adults, bilateral, multiple, linked to allergy and asthma (aspirin sensitivity). Antrochoanal polyp: children and young adults, single, unilateral, from the maxillary sinus growing back to the choana. Polyps are insensitive to touch and do not bleed.

Epistaxis

High-yieldRepeated23 past questions

Most nosebleeds come from Little's area (Kiesselbach plexus) on the front of the septum: pinch the soft nose and lean forward. Posterior bleeds come from the sphenopalatine artery, the 'artery of epistaxis' (Woodruff plexus). Recurrent bleeding in an adolescent boy suggests juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma.

Answer: B. The sphenopalatine artery is called the artery of epistaxis.

Mucormycosis

High-yieldRepeatedINI-CET19 past questions

Rhino-orbito-cerebral mucormycosis: uncontrolled diabetes (ketoacidosis), steroids. Black necrotic crust in the nose or palate, eye swelling, cranial nerve palsies. Broad aseptate hyphae at right angles. Treat with urgent surgical debridement plus amphotericin B and control of sugar.

Answer: A. Spreading sinus and orbital infection in a diabetic points to mucormycosis.

Larynx 90 questions

Laryngeal carcinoma

Must-doRepeated21 past questions

Glottic cancer is the commonest and has the best outlook: early hoarseness and few lymph nodes. Smoking is the main risk. T1a: one cord with normal movement; T1b: both cords; T2: spread above or below or reduced movement; T3: fixed cord. Early stages: radiotherapy or laser surgery.

Try a past question

  1. A. T1a
  2. B. T1b
  3. C. T2
  4. D. T3

Answer: A. A growth on one vocal cord with normal movement is T1a.

Recurrent laryngeal nerve injury

High-yieldRepeated9 past questions

The left recurrent laryngeal nerve loops under the aortic arch beside the ligamentum arteriosum, so it is at risk in PDA surgery, aortic aneurysm, enlarged left atrium (Ortner syndrome) and lung cancer. The right loops under the subclavian artery.

Vocal cord paralysis

High-yieldRepeated17 past questions

The external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve supplies the cricothyroid (tightens the cords for high notes); injured in thyroid surgery near the superior thyroid artery. The recurrent laryngeal nerve supplies all other muscles; unilateral injury gives hoarseness, bilateral gives stridor.

  3. C. Recurrent laryngeal nerve
  4. D. Intubation granuloma

Answer: A. Losing high notes after thyroid surgery means injury to the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve.

Hoarseness evaluation

High-yieldRepeated15 past questions

Vocal nodules (singer's nodes): voice abuse, both cords at the junction of the front third and back two-thirds; voice therapy. Reinke oedema: heavy smokers, both cords swollen, deep voice. Hoarseness for more than 3 weeks in a smoker needs laryngoscopy to rule out cancer.

  3. C. Reinke's edema
  4. D. Laryngeal papillomatosis

Answer: C. A deep, hoarse voice in a heavy smoker points to Reinke's oedema.

Airway obstruction / stridor

High-yieldRepeated32 past questions

Laryngomalacia: the commonest cause of stridor in newborns; omega-shaped epiglottis, worse lying on the back, usually settles by 18-24 months. Croup: steeple sign. Epiglottitis: thumb sign. Inspiratory stridor = above the cords; expiratory = in the trachea or bronchi.

Head & Neck 84 questions

Thyroid swelling

High-yieldRepeated3 past questions

A solitary thyroid nodule is checked with ultrasound and FNAC. Papillary cancer: Orphan Annie eye nuclei, psammoma bodies, lymph node spread, the best outlook. Follicular: needs capsule or vessel invasion to call it cancer, so FNAC cannot tell.

  1. A. Follicular carcinoma
  2. B. Medullary carcinoma
  3. C. Papillary carcinoma
  4. D. Anaplastic carcinoma

Answer: C. Orphan Annie nuclei and psammoma bodies on FNAC mean papillary carcinoma.

Branchial cyst, thyroglossal cyst

High-yieldRepeated16 past questions

Thyroglossal cyst: midline, moves up when the tongue is put out; Sistrunk operation (removes the middle of the hyoid). Branchial cyst: from the second cleft, at the front edge of the sternocleidomastoid between the upper and middle thirds; cholesterol crystals in the fluid.

Neck node levels

High-yieldRepeated13 past questions

Neck node levels: I submental and submandibular, II-IV along the internal jugular (upper, middle, lower), V posterior triangle, VI central. A hard node in an adult over 40 is cancer until proven otherwise; look for the primary (nasopharynx, tonsil, tongue base).

Salivary gland tumours

High-yieldRepeated18 past questions

Pleomorphic adenoma: the commonest salivary tumour, in the parotid; recurs if not fully removed (superficial parotidectomy). Warthin tumour: older male smokers, both sides, hot spot on a technetium scan. Mucoepidermoid carcinoma: the commonest malignant one. Adenoid cystic: spreads along nerves.

Parotid surgery (facial nerve)

High-yieldRepeated13 past questions

Landmarks for finding the facial nerve in parotid surgery: the tragal pointer (nerve about 1 cm deep and below), the posterior belly of digastric, the tympanomastoid suture, the styloid process. Frey syndrome (gustatory sweating) can follow parotidectomy.

Instruments & Surgery 30 questions

Myringoplasty vs Tympanoplasty vs Mastoidectomy

AskedRepeated6 past questions

Myringoplasty repairs the drum only; tympanoplasty repairs the drum and middle ear bones; mastoidectomy clears the mastoid (cortical for acute mastoiditis, modified radical for cholesteatoma). Macewen's (suprameatal) triangle marks the mastoid antrum, about 1.5 cm deep.

Tracheostomy indications

AskedRepeated9 past questions

Indications: upper airway obstruction, long-term ventilation, clearing secretions. Use a cuffed tube for ventilated patients (seals the airway, prevents aspiration). Done through the 2nd-4th tracheal rings; avoid the first ring (stenosis). Emergency airway: cricothyroidotomy.

Endoscopic sinus surgery basics

AskedRepeated5 past questions

Functional endoscopic sinus surgery restores drainage through the ostiomeatal complex. The first step is uncinectomy (removing the uncinate process), then opening the bulla ethmoidalis. Risks: orbital injury, CSF leak.

Try a past question

  3. C. Superior turbinate
  4. D. Sphenoid sinus ostium

Answer: A. FESS starts by removing the uncinate process to open the ostiomeatal complex.
